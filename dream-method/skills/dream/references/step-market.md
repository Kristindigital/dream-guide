# M: Market

**Get it in front of the right people.** Their product is for sale. Now help the right people see it, want it and buy it, without needing a big following, a viral post or social media.
The goal of this step: *"I know who I'm talking to, where to find them, what to say, and what I'm doing for the next 30 days."*

This step is Kristin's Market method.
**Marketing here is every way to get their product in front of their buyers, mostly without social media:** Etsy search, Pinterest, an email list, people they already know, communities, partnerships, Google, Skool, more marketplaces (Amazon KDP, Printify, Teachers Pay Teachers, Creative Market), and calls or lives. Setting up a social media profile and basic sharing are part of this step (see "If they want social media" in Round 2). A full social media strategy is Kristin's separate social media add-on. Everything else in this step works without posting on Instagram, TikTok or Facebook, and without filming.

**You do the work.** You draft the map, the messages, the pins, the emails and the plan from their notes. They approve, and they do the clicks for their own accounts.
Read `market-places.md` for the place they choose, and `audits.md` whenever you check a page a buyer sees. Read `why-people-buy.md` (sections 7, 9 to 12, 14 and 15) before writing anything a buyer will read, and `rules.md` before every claim.

## Before you start: the readiness check

Market needs, from Apply: **where it's sold and the live listing link** (SKILL.md, the readiness check). Open the link.
- **Not live yet, or the link doesn't open:** finish that part of Apply first, in one line ("Let's get it live first, so everyone we send there can buy it.").
- **Hidden on purpose** (they want it hidden until launch day): that's fine. Planning before launch is smart. Note it in their notes, build the map, the places and the messages, and make sure it's live before anything sends people to it: the setup in Round 5, pins, emails and the first day of the 30-day plan. Check the link again then (Kristin's test, Oct 17).
- **You can't open the page** (Etsy often blocks automated visits): that's fine. You'll see it through their screenshots in Round 3 (`audits.md`).

Also check their notes for **"Hours I can give each week"** and **"On camera"**. Ask only what's missing, in one message:

> Two quick questions so your plan fits your real life:
> 1. **How many hours a week can you give to marketing?** (1 to 2 is fine.)
> 2. **Do you have people already?** Friends, family, an email list, past buyers, a group you're part of. Even 20 people counts.

Then open the step with Kristin's welcome, in a few short lines:

> **Your product is ready. Now let's get it in front of the right people.**
> You don't need a huge following. You don't need to go viral. You don't need to be an influencer.
> You need the right people to see it, a clear reason to care, and a simple plan you'll actually stick to.

## Round 1: Your marketing map (from your Research)

Marketing in one line: **getting the right product in front of the right people, with the right message.** Every sale goes the same way: they **see** it, they **want** it, they **buy** it.
Kristin's line for this round: *your research is now your marketing map.* Don't ask them to fill in a worksheet. Draft it from their notes, then show it.

**1. Who they are before they find you.** From Research: their stage of life, a normal day, what's taking their time and energy, what's hard right now. Three or four short lines.

**2. Their real words.** Up to 10 phrases their buyer actually uses, copied from "My buyer in their own words" in Research, word for word. Kristin's rule: *what does she actually say? Not what you think she says.* If Research found fewer than 10, use what's there and say so.

**3. The gap.** Where they are now (frustrating, hard, overwhelming) → what they want instead. This is their Before/After promise, in the buyer's words (`why-people-buy.md`, section 9).

**4. The four stages, one message each.** Not every message needs to sell. People need different things at different stages.

| Stage | What they're thinking | What they need from us | Message shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | "Something feels hard, but I haven't named it." | To feel seen | "I see what you're feeling." |
| **Problem-aware** | "I know what's wrong. I need help." | One small, helpful step | "Here's why this keeps happening." |
| **Solution-aware** | "There are solutions. Which one is right for me?" | To see what's possible | "Here's a simpler way." |
| **Ready to buy** | "Show me the clear next step." | A clear invitation: who it's for, what it helps with, what's inside, the price, where to buy | "Here's your next step." |

Write one short message per stage, in the buyer's words. Kristin's example for a dinner planner, Unaware: *"Dinner isn't stressful because you're bad at planning. It's stressful because you're making too many decisions at the end of a long day."*

**5. Their journey statement:**
> I help **{specific person}** who is struggling with **{current problem}** move toward **{desired result}** through **{their product}**.

Show the whole map on one screen, then ask:

> **Here's your marketing map.** It's built from your research, so it's in your buyer's own words.
> Does it sound like them? Say **yes**, or tell me what to change.

On yes, save it in their notes ("My marketing map"). It feeds every message, pin and email in this step.

## Round 2: Where you'll market

**Start with one main place.** Kristin's rule: *you don't need to be everywhere.* One place they'll stick with beats five they abandon. Their main place is their weekly habit. Once it's set up, they can set up more places right away (Round 5b), so they feel ready to launch. Those start small, and a second full weekly habit comes once the first one sticks (`keep-growing.md`).
Their first goal is the right people, not the most people. *100 people who need it beat 10,000 random views.*

**The places** (setups in `market-places.md`):

| Group | Places | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **Search:** people are already looking | Etsy search, Pinterest, Google | Their product is something people type into a search bar ("meal planner printable"). |
| **Your own people** | An email list (with a freebie), people you already know, past buyers | They have a few people already, or they want an audience no app can take away. |
| **Communities and partners** | Facebook groups, subreddits and forums where their buyer asks questions, other sellers with the same buyer, guest spots, local spots (libraries, schools, parent groups) | Research found their buyer talking in a specific place, or they like helping one person at a time. |
| **Skool** | Their own community, Skool's Discovery and neighboring communities | They sell on Skool. |
| **More marketplaces:** buyers already shopping there | Amazon (a printed version through KDP), Teachers Pay Teachers, Creative Market, Printify products in their shop | Their product fits one: a planner or journal (Amazon), a classroom resource (Teachers Pay Teachers), Canva templates (Creative Market), designs that work on shirts or mugs (Printify). Usually a second place. |
| **Social media content** | Instagram, Facebook, YouTube | They want to make content regularly. (See "If they want social media" below.) |

**First, ask what feels like them** (Kristin, Oct 17: their input comes first). One short message, before you recommend anything:

> Before I pick your main place, which of these sounds most like you?
> (a) **Being found in search:** people type what they need into Pinterest, Etsy or Google, and find your product.
> (b) **My own people and groups:** friends, family, Facebook groups I'm in, or an email list.
> (c) **Other marketplaces:** places where buyers already shop, like Amazon for a printed planner, Teachers Pay Teachers or Creative Market.
> (d) **Social media:** sharing it where I already post, like Instagram or Facebook.
> (e) **I'm not sure.** Let's talk it through.
>
> Just reply with a letter.

If they've already told you where they'd like to market, start from that and skip this message. Having people (the readiness question) isn't the same as choosing them, so still ask.
- **Their pick fits:** go with it. Say why it fits them in one line, from their notes.
- **It works, but another fits much better:** say so honestly, once, with the reason (`rules.md`, honest feedback). For example, a brand new Stan seller who picks (a): Stan doesn't bring shoppers the way Etsy search does, so search there means Pinterest. Then go with their choice if they still want it.
- **It doesn't fit their product** (Teachers Pay Teachers for a product that isn't for teachers, for example): say it kindly, and recommend the best fit.
- **(d) Social media:** follow "If they want social media" below. Help with the basic setup (their shop link in their bio, and one "I made this!" launch post or Story), then the add-on line once, or the hand-off if it's in this chat. Then recommend one main place that works without posting every day (Kristin, Oct 17).
- **(e), or they're unsure:** ask one easy question: "Which sounds most like something you'd do each week: make a few pins, write to people one by one, or answer questions in a group?" Then recommend from their answer and their notes.

**Then recommend one,** based on their answer and their notes. Don't hand them the whole menu to decide alone. Kristin's four questions decide it:
1. **Where is their buyer already?** (Research: where the quotes came from, where similar products sell.)
2. **What does the product need?** Something people search for fits search. Something you need to see used fits Pinterest or a demo.
3. **What will they do every week, realistically?** (Their hours, and "On camera".)
4. **Where can a brand new seller get found?**

Good starting matches (a suggestion, never a rule):
- **Etsy sellers:** Etsy search is already working for them. Add **Pinterest** as their main place to send more people in.
- **Stan Store or Beacons:** these shops are built to be shared, so they need a place that sends people: **their own people** (email list and people they know), **Pinterest**, or **communities**.
- **Systeme.io, Payhip or Gumroad:** an **email list with a freebie** as their main place, with Pinterest or communities to fill it.
- **Skool:** their community plus Discovery (`skool.md`), and neighboring communities.
- **They already have people** (a list, a group, past buyers): start there. It's the fastest way to a first sale.
- **Teachers and homeschool sellers:** Teachers Pay Teachers can be their main place (`market-places.md`, section 10). Check the exclusive rule against where else they sell.
- **Planners, journals and workbooks:** once their main place is running, a printed version on Amazon through KDP is a strong second place.

Show your recommendation in a few lines: the place, why it fits them (from their notes), and what a normal week looks like. Then:

> **Your main place: {place}.** {why, in one line from their notes}
> Want to start there? Say **yes**, or tell me which place feels better.

Save it with Kristin's sentence: **"My main place is {place} because my buyer {reason}."**

**Selling on calls or lives:** if they sell a course, membership, community, coaching or a higher-priced offer, or they'd like to go live to invite people in, add it to their plan with `sales-calls.md`. It works alongside their main place (the place brings people in; the call or live is where they decide).

### If they want social media

**The basic setup is part of this step** (Kristin, Oct 17). Help with it here, as part of "People you already know" (`market-places.md`, section 4), never as their main place. You write the words; they do the clicks in their own account:
1. **One platform** where their buyer already spends time. Kristin's materials cover Instagram, Facebook and YouTube.
2. **Name:** their shop or brand name, plus what they help with, for example "EasyDinnerMama | Easy dinner planning".
3. **Username:** the same as their shop name if it's free, so buyers find the same name everywhere.
4. **Profile photo:** a clear, friendly photo of them, or their logo.
5. **Bio:** 3 short lines in their buyer's words: who they help, what they offer, and one next step ("Get the dinner planner ↓").
6. **Link in bio:** their product link, or their shop link on Stan or Beacons (those are made for a bio link).
7. **Their first post:** one "I made this!" launch post or Story: a hook from `hooks.md`, what it is and who it's for in their buyer's words, and the link (adapt their warm-launch message).
If their screen looks different, ask what they see. Buttons change, so check the platform's help page before you name one.

**A full social media strategy is not part of this step.** That means what to post each week, content pillars, Reels and filming, growing followers, and DM automations. It's Kristin's separate social media add-on, sold separately. It covers Instagram, Facebook and YouTube (not TikTok).
If they ask about TikTok, say plainly that Kristin's materials don't cover it, and recommend one of the places above.
- **If Kristin's social media add-on is available in this chat** (a skill named `dream-content`), tell them in one line that social media strategy is its specialty, and hand off to it. It reads the same business notes, so they won't repeat anything.
- **If it isn't,** help with the basic setup above, then say this once, warmly (Kristin's words, Oct 17):
  > Social media is one of the biggest free ways to get eyes on your products. If you want the exact methods Kristin used to grow to over 1 million followers across her social platforms within one year, they're coming in her separate **social media add-on**. You don't need it to make your first sale.
  > For now, let's pick a place that works without posting every day, so your product gets seen this week.
  Then recommend a main place that works without posting every day. Say it once per chat, never pitch it again, and never make them feel they're missing something they need. Kristin's numbers are her own results, never a promise of theirs.

## Round 3: Get found (make it easy to understand)

Before sending anyone to the listing, make sure it works. *Great marketing can't fix a product nobody understands.*

**Check their live page with `audits.md`:** see it for real (their link, or up to 4 phone screenshots), go through the list for their page (an Etsy listing, a sales page, a storefront), and report the 3 fixes that will help most. If they also have a freebie page or Pinterest already, check those once their main place is set up (Round 5). The heart of the check:

**1. The 5-second check.** Look at their live listing as a stranger would, on a phone. In 5 seconds, can someone tell:
- **Who** it's for?
- **What** it helps them do?
- **What's included?**
- **How** it works (what they get after paying)?
- **Why** it's worth it?

**2. Clear beats clever.** The title says what it is and who it's for, with the main keyword first (`why-people-buy.md`, section 15). Kristin's example: not "Sunday Reset", but "Sunday Reset Planner for Busy Moms".

**3. Their buyer's words, not ours.** Check the first lines of the description, the main image and the tags (Etsy) against their keyword set and their 10 phrases.

**4. The platform's own settings** for being found, from `market-places.md` (for example Etsy's Share & Save and Offsite Ads choice, Pinterest's profile, Skool's Discovery description).

Report it the short way (`audits.md`, section 3): what's working, the 3 fixes that come first (each written out, ready to paste), and anything for later. Approved work is protected: suggest changes and let them choose. If everything passes, say so in one line and move on. Save it under **Page checks** in their notes.

## Round 4: What to say (your message)

*Attention isn't the sale.* Someone can see it, click it and still not buy. The message is what helps the right person think "this is exactly what I need."

Build their **message bank** from their map, their promise and their buyer's words. Write it in their tone ("My tone"):

1. **Problem → solution → product** (3 versions). Start with what's hard, show a simpler way, then connect it to the product.
   *"I kept forgetting what to prep for the week. So I made a simple planner. Now it's all on one page."* (Only true stories, in their own words. Ask before using anything personal.)
2. **Before → after** (3 versions). Their promise in different words. The product is the bridge.
3. **Features → benefits** (5 lines). What it *has*, turned into what it *does* for them.
   Feature: "50 pages." Benefit: "50 ready-to-use pages, so you don't start from scratch."
4. **One clear next step** (their call to action): short, plain and the same everywhere. "Get the planner." "Grab the free list."
   For opening lines (a pin headline, a community post, a launch message), write 3 options with `hooks.md`.
5. **Honest proof.** No reviews yet? Write **"what you can expect"** instead (`rules.md`, rule 3). Show the product itself: a peek inside, how it works.
   Their first proof comes from real buyers. After a sale, a short thank-you message asking what they used most is fine. **Never trade anything for a review.** Etsy bans rewards for positive reviews, and our rule is simpler: no rewards at all. Only share a buyer's words with their permission.

Selling without feeling salesy (`why-people-buy.md`, section 12): if it truly helps, sharing it is helping. Marketing means making the offer, not only teaching forever.

Show the bank, get their OK, and save the best line of each kind in their notes ("My messages").

## Round 5: Set up your main place

Open `market-places.md` and follow the setup for their main place, **3 to 5 steps per message**, waiting for "done" or a screenshot each time. They create their own accounts and click their own buttons; you write everything and build every graphic in their Canva, in their look (style sheet).

What you'll usually make here:
- **Pinterest:** their profile words, 2 or 3 boards, and their first 5 pins (designs plus titles and descriptions).
- **Email list:** the freebie (built in Canva, `upsells-and-funnels.md`, part 2), the sign-up page, the welcome emails and a launch email.
- **People you know:** their launch message, ready to personalize, and their list of who to tell.
- **Communities:** 2 or 3 places to join, the rules for each, and their first helpful answers.
- **Partnerships:** 5 people or businesses to reach out to, and the first message.
- **Etsy search:** the listing fixes from Round 3, Share & Save, and their Offsite Ads choice.
- **Calls or lives** (if they're selling that way): their booking setup, their call questions, their deck and price slide in Canva, and a practice call (`sales-calls.md`).

**Every message that sends people uses their real listing link** from their notes, typed in, never a `{your link}` blank. If their notes only have the shop link, ask once for the product's own link. If the product is hidden until launch, say so in one line: "Send these once your planner is visible, on launch day." (Kristin's test, Oct 17.)
Never say something is live, saved or sent until it is (`rules.md`, rule 4). Save each link in their notes as it goes live.

## Round 5b: More places (optional)

Kristin wants every student to feel ready to launch and get eyes on their product (Oct 17). So once their main place is set up, offer to set up another, one at a time:

> Your {main place} is ready. ✓ Want to set up another place to get your {product} seen? These fit yours:
> (a) **Pinterest:** I'll design your first 5 pins in your Canva and write their titles and descriptions. A good pin can keep sending people to your product for months.
> (b) **An email list:** a free gift, like {a freebie idea}, that brings {buyers} onto your list, plus your welcome emails.
> (c) **{A marketplace or partner idea that fits}:** for example, a printed version on Amazon through KDP.
> (d) **Not now.** Let's make my 30-day plan.
>
> ⭐ **My pick: {one},** because {one reason from their notes}.

- **Only places that fit** their product, platform and buyer, up to 4, never one they've already set up. Choose from: Pinterest pins, an email list with a freebie, **Facebook groups and other communities** (help first, `market-places.md` section 5), partners and local spots (section 6), **their social media profile** ("If they want social media" in Round 2), more marketplaces (section 10) and Google (section 7, for someone who likes writing).
- **Pick honestly for their hours.** If they have about 2 hours a week or less, the pick is (d), with one line: "One place done well beats two half-done. We can add this after your first month." It's still their call.
- **For a new seller with a printable or planner, Pinterest is often the best extra:** the pins are made once and can keep working (`market-places.md` section 2).
- **Build it like Round 5:** follow `market-places.md` for that place, 3 to 5 steps per message.
- **After each one, ask again,** with only the places not yet built. Stop when they choose "Not now".
- **Save each place in their notes** ("Also set up: …"). Every place goes into their 30-day plan.

## Round 6: Your first 30 days

Kristin's line: *you don't need a complicated strategy. For the next 30 days, your job is simple: get seen, get interest, get sales.*

Build their plan from their main place and their hours. Each week has one focus:

| Week | Focus | What it means for them |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Get ready** | Finish the setup | Anything left from Rounds 3 to 5. Their link works. Their message is clear. |
| **2. Get seen** | Show up in their place | The weekly activity for their place (`market-places.md`), for example 5 pins, 5 helpful answers, or 10 personal messages. |
| **3. Promote** | Talk about the product directly | Show it, explain who it's for and what it solves, and **ask for the sale.** Many beginners teach forever and never ask. |
| **4. Review and improve** | Look at what happened | What got seen, what got clicks, what got replies, what sold. Change one thing (`keep-growing.md`). |

**Every place they set up goes in the plan.** Their main place gets its full weekly activity. Each extra place from Round 5b gets its smallest weekly version (for example, 2 new pins a week), so the plan still fits their hours.

Also give them:
- **The busy-week version:** the smallest version of their week that still counts (for example 2 pins and 1 email). Done beats perfect.
- **Their number.** Kristin's question: *how many tries will you make before you decide it doesn't work?* Help them pick a real number (for example "30 pins", "20 helpful answers", "15 personal messages") and save it. A quiet first week is information, not a verdict.
- **Their first goal: the first sale.** Not 10,000 followers, not a viral post. The first sale teaches them what works.

Keep the plan in their notes, as a short list. Don't build a spreadsheet, tracker or calendar app (`rules.md`, rule 10).

>

On a clear yes:
1. Mark Market approved with today's date. Set "My next step" to their first week's task, and their first check-in date (7 days from now).
2. Celebrate, because it's big: **they've done all five steps.** Define, Research, Execute, Apply and Market. Most people never get this far.
3. Show the progress card with all five steps ✓.
4. Their notes (saved, or the keep-going note if you can't save them), then this handoff:

> **Your next step: your first week.** {their week 1 task in one line}
> In a week, come back to this chat in your DREAM project and type **Keep going**. I'll ask how it went, look at what's working, and help you change one thing.

Then, in the same message, **more ways to get seen, whenever you're ready** (Kristin, Oct 17): 3 to 5 that fit their product and that they haven't set up, one line each, with what to type. For example:

> **More ways to get seen, whenever you're ready:**
> - **Pinterest pins:** people search Pinterest for ideas like yours. Type "Make 5 Pinterest pins for my product."
> - **A free gift for an email list:** so interested buyers can hear from you again. Type "Make a freebie for my email list."

If they haven't set up a social media profile, you can list it ("Set up my social media profile."), with the add-on line only if it hasn't come up in this chat.
