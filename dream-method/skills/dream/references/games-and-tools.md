# Games and tracking tools (playable products)

A **playable product** is one web page that opens in any web browser on a phone, a tablet or a computer, with nothing to install. There are two kinds:
- **Games:** kids and family games they tap and play, like a board game where you draw a card and hop along the path, with the cards read out loud.
- **Tracking tools:** charts that tick, count and save, like a chore chart with stars, a habit tracker or a savings challenge.

Kristin's pick is always **printable + playable**: the student makes the printable in Canva first (Execute, as usual), then you build the playable version from that same art. Buyers get both ways to play, and the student has a bundle to sell (Apply, Round 3).

Read this file when:
- their product is a game or a tracking tool (blueprint 9 in `execute-blueprints.md`), or
- they say yes to **"Make it playable"** in Execute ("Make it interactive?" in `step-execute.md`), or
- they ask **"Make my product playable"**, "make a game", "turn my chart into an app", at any point.

---

## 1. What fits, and what doesn't

**Games that work well as a first version:**
- A path board game: draw a color card or roll a die, hop along the path, special spaces and picture cards, first one to the finish wins.
- Memory match, bingo with a caller, I-spy or a scavenger hunt, a spin-the-wheel or card-flip party game (charades, would you rather, conversation cards), a quiz with cheers.
- Learning games for kids: sight words, letters, numbers, matching, with the words read out loud.

**Tracking tools that work well as a first version:**
- A chore or reward chart: tap to add a star, and a prize when the stars add up.
- A habit, reading, water or workout tracker: tap each day, see the streak.
- A savings challenge: tap to color in each amount, with the total that adds itself up.
- A countdown or advent calendar: tap a door each day.

**Keep the first version small:** one game with one way to play, or one tracker with one job. More modes, levels and themes are version 2 (save them in Decisions).

**What a playable product can't do,** said honestly in one or two lines, with the closest thing you can build:
- logins or accounts, or saving to the cloud
- syncing between devices or family members (progress saves on the device it's played on)
- playing together online from different phones (everyone plays on one device, passing it around)
- payments inside it, or an app in the App Store or Google Play

## 2. Their own game, never a copy

Every game is theirs: their own name, characters, board and look.
- **"Like Candy Land" is a fine shape to borrow** ("draw a color, hop to it"). The brand name never goes in the title, tags or description, or the listing gets pulled. The same goes for UNO, Monopoly, Guess Who, Connect 4, Pictionary, Yahtzee and every other brand.
- **No brand characters or art:** no Disney, Bluey, Pokémon, Paw Patrol or any other show, game or toy, in the art, the name or the tags.
- **Classic games anyone can make are fine:** memory, bingo, tic-tac-toe, hangman, charades, would you rather, I-spy, scavenger hunts, matching.
- Their art comes from their Canva printable (`execute-blueprints.md`, "Licensing", applies), or from shapes and pictures you draw in code. Never use art from the internet.

If they ask for a copy, say it kindly in one line and offer their own version: "Let's make it yours, with its own name and characters, so your listing is safe."

## 3. The brief: everything in one message

Ask everything the playable version needs **in one message, with your pick filled in** (`rules.md`, rules 20 to 23). Take what their notes and their printable already answer, and skip those questions. Their How to Play page usually answers most of a game's rules: read it first.

**For a game:**
> ✨ Let's make your {game} playable! Here's my plan, with my picks filled in:
> 1. **Who plays:** {1 to 4 players} on one phone or iPad, passed around. Each player picks {their characters}.
> 2. **How you move:** {from their How to Play page, for example "tap the deck, it flips to a color, and your piece hops to the next space of that color"}.
> 3. **Special spaces and cards:** {theirs, for example "the slide is a shortcut, and picture cards send you to that spot"}.
> 4. **Read out loud:** ⭐ yes, every card is read out loud, so little ones who can't read yet can play. {Skip for adults.}
> 5. **Sounds:** ⭐ a little sound on every hop, plus a cheer for the winner, with a sound on/off button.
> 6. **The win:** ⭐ confetti and a "{their winner words}" screen, with Play again.
> 7. **The look:** your printable's own board and characters, so the two match.
>
> Say **yes** to all of it, or tell me what to change.

**For a tracking tool:**
> ✨ Let's make your {tracker} playable! Here's my plan, with my picks filled in:
> 1. **What it tracks:** {chores, habits, reading, savings}, {daily or weekly}.
> 2. **Who uses it:** {kids or grown-ups}. Buyers type in {their children's names / their habits} the first time they open it.
> 3. **Tapping:** tap a box to add a {star / tick / color}, and tap again to undo.
> 4. **The reward:** ⭐ {for example "5 stars unlocks a prize the parent types in"}, with a little cheer.
> 5. **Saving:** it saves on the device it's used on, and a **Start a new week** button clears it (after "Are you sure?").
> 6. **Sounds:** ⭐ a soft sound on each tap, with a sound on/off button.
> 7. **The look:** your printable's own pages, colors and icons.
>
> Say **yes** to all of it, or tell me what to change.

Save their answers in their notes (Execute, "Playable version") before you build.

## 4. The art: from their Canva printable

The playable version uses the same art as their printable, so the two match and the listing photos tell one story.

**Get the pages as pictures.** Ask them for PNGs in the same message that starts the build:
> Then download your printable once more as pictures: in Canva, **Share** → **Download** → **PNG** → **All pages** → **Download**. Open the zip and drag all the pictures in here.

(If you can export from their Canva and open the export yourself, do that instead and skip the ask. On many setups you can't open Canva's download link, so asking is the safe default.)

**What you need from the pictures:**
- **The board or chart** as the background, kept sharp (resize it for screens, never stretch it).
- **The pieces, characters, icons and stickers,** cut out with clean edges (no leftover background around them). Look at every cutout as a picture and redo any that lost a part or kept a box around it.
- **For a path game:** every space on the path, in order from start to finish, with its color. Find them with code, then **draw a numbered dot on every space, on top of the board, and look at that picture.** Fix any space that's missing, doubled, out of order or the wrong color. Picture cards go to the space nearest their spot on the board. Shortcuts follow the line drawn on the board.
- **Check the board itself:** if the path doesn't end at the finish, a shortcut doesn't start on a space, or a word is cut off at the edge, tell them in one line and say how the game handles it. Offer to fix the printable in Canva too, so both versions match, as its own question after the brief. Never change their Canva design on the brief's "yes": a Canva change always gets its own preview and its own yes (`step-execute.md`, Round 1).

## 5. Building it

You need your own file-creation tools for this (Claude's **Create and edit files** setting). Without them, follow "Without file-creation tools" (section 9).

**One file, ready for anywhere:**
- One self-contained web page: `index.html`, with every picture, sound and word inside it. No outside scripts, no outside pictures, no sound files from elsewhere. Fonts: one Google font is fine, with a safe fallback, so it still looks right offline.
- Pictures compressed for screens (WebP or JPG), so the whole file stays **under 5 MB**, and 1 to 3 MB is best: it opens fast on a phone, and it fits every free host (some free hosts stop at 5 MB per file).
- Put it in a folder named `{Product-Name}-Playable` and zip that folder: `{Product-Name}-Playable.zip`. The folder (with `index.html` inside) is what goes online; the zip is the backup download.

**Made for little hands and small screens:**
- Works on a phone and an iPad, held upright or sideways, with nothing hanging off the side of the screen. The board fits the screen; nothing scrolls sideways.
- Big tap areas (at least 48 px), big clear words (at least 16 px), and a clear "whose turn" or "what to do" line at all times.
- A title screen with the product name and their shop name, a **How to play** button (their How to Play page), and a **New game** or **Start over** button.

**Voice and sounds:**
- **Read out loud** with the browser's own voice (speech synthesis). Phones only allow sound after the first tap, so start the voice and sounds from the first tap (the "Let's go" button). A voice on/off button.
- **Sounds** made in code (Web Audio): soft, short and happy, never loud. A sound on/off button.
- **Confetti and cheers** for the win. Respect "reduce motion": no flying confetti for people who turned motion off; show a still celebration instead.

**Saving (tracking tools):**
- Save progress on the device (the browser's local storage), so it's still there tomorrow. Say it plainly on the How to use page and in the listing: **"Your progress saves on this device, in this browser."**
- Every "clear" or "start over" asks "Are you sure?" inside the page first.

**Safe for kids:** no links out of the game, no ads, nothing collected or sent anywhere. Names typed in stay on the device. Their shop link goes on their thank-you PDF, not inside a kids' game.

**On the free plan,** build it in pieces so a pause never loses work (free Claude pauses for a few hours when they've used their share). Before you start, tell them once:
> This is a bigger build, so free Claude may pause once or twice. That's normal, and nothing is lost: come back to this chat and type **Keep going**.

Then: (1) the cut-out art and the numbered-dot path picture, (2) the game, (3) the finishing touches. Hand over the file after each piece, so the newest version is always in the chat. Keep your messages short.

## 6. Check it before you hand it over

**If you can open it in a web browser yourself,** play it: a whole game from start to finish (or a full week of a tracker, closing and reopening it), at phone size and iPad size, held both ways. Fix what breaks, then play it again.

**If you can't open it in a browser** (most chats can't), test it with code instead:
- Play 200 games in code: every game ends, nobody gets stuck, every card and space does what it should, and the shortcut and picture cards land on the right spaces.
- Look at the numbered-dot picture of the path one more time.
- For a tracker: tick, save, reload and check that everything stayed.

Then say honestly what you checked and what you couldn't (for example "I couldn't hear the sounds or the voice"), and give them **their check**. A student playing it once is what catches the last problems.

## 7. The handover (the wow moment)

> ✨ **Your {product} is ready to play!** Here it is: {the file, and the zip}
> **Try it:** {2 or 3 things, for example "pick two players, tap the deck, and watch your piece hop. Then keep drawing until someone lands on a shortcut"}.
> **Your check,** before you sell it (about 10 minutes):
> 1. Play one whole game {tracker: tap a full day, close it and open it again} on your phone or iPad.
> 2. Try {each special card or space / the reward and Start a new week}.
> 3. Turn the sound on. No sound on an iPhone? Check it isn't on silent.
> 4. Turn your phone sideways.
>
> Tell me anything that looks wrong, and I'll fix it. Then tell me which devices you played it on. Your listing will name only those.

Fix what they find, hand over the new file, and ask them to try that one part again. Then save it in their notes (Execute, "Playable version": how it plays, the file name, the devices they tested, the date). After Apply, add the Netlify link.

**Changed something later?** Make the change, hand over the new file, and remind them to put the new folder online the same way (section 8, "Updating it"), so buyers' link shows the new version.

## 8. Getting it to buyers (Apply)

A playable product is a web page, so buyers open it with a **link**. A file opened from a phone's Files app often won't play its sounds or taps, and Etsy doesn't accept web page files at all. So the student puts it online free with **Netlify**, and buyers get the link (plus the zip as a backup) in a thank-you PDF.

**Put it online, free** (Netlify's own pages, checked November 2026; screens change, so if what they see doesn't match, ask what they see). 3 to 5 steps per message, then wait for "done" or a screenshot:
1. **Make a free Netlify account** at netlify.com (**Sign up**, with their email or Google). Sign in first: a site dropped without an account is only temporary.
2. **Unzip** `{Product-Name}-Playable.zip`, so they have the folder with `index.html` inside.
3. Go to **app.netlify.com/drop** and drag the whole folder onto the page. In a moment, it's online.
4. **If it says the project is private,** click **Make public** (new Netlify accounts start private). If they can't find it: the project's **Project configuration** → **General** → **Visitor access** → **Project visibility** → public.
5. **Give it a nice name:** in the project's settings, change the project name to their product name (for example `rainbow-race-game`), so the link reads `rainbow-race-game.netlify.app`.
6. **Test the link** on their phone, signed out of Netlify: it opens, it plays, it makes sounds.

**Updating it:** in Netlify, open the project, go to **Deploys**, and drag the new folder onto the drop area under **Production deploys**. The link stays the same, so every buyer gets the new version.

**Be honest with them:** anyone with the link can open it, the same as any digital download. Netlify's free plan has a monthly allowance: roughly 7,000 opens a month for a 2 MB game (each re-upload uses a little of it too). If it runs out, every site on their account shows "Site not available" until the next month. They can see their usage in their Netlify account, and a game that sells that well is a great problem to have: Netlify's paid plan lifts the limit.

**Delivering it** (`delivery.md`, "Route F", then "Bundles, step by step"): a thank-you PDF made in Canva, with:
- a **"Play now"** button (the Netlify link), with one line under it: "Opens in your web browser on any phone, tablet or computer. Tap the screen once to turn on the sound."
- a **"Download your printable"** button (Google Drive link), and
- a **"Download the game file"** button (the zip, on Google Drive), with one line under it: "A backup copy. Unzip it and open index.html in your web browser."

**On Etsy:** upload the thank-you PDF and the printable PDF as the listing's files. The link inside the PDF goes to the product they bought, which is fine on Etsy. Nothing in the PDF links to buying somewhere else (`storefronts.md`, Etsy).

**The listing** (Apply, Round 3): say what it does in buyer words ("tap the deck and your piece hops along the path", "reads every card out loud", "tap to add a star"). Say **"plays in your web browser on any phone, tablet or computer, no app to download"**. Never call it an app from the App Store. Name only the devices they tested. Trackers: "Your progress saves on this device." Mockups: screenshots of it on their phone or iPad, placed in a device mockup in Canva, next to the printable (Round 4).

## 9. Without file-creation tools

A playable version needs Claude's **Create and edit files** setting. Ask them to turn it on with the message in `step-execute.md` ("Without your own file-creation tools"). If they can't (for example, a work account blocks it) or would rather not, say so kindly and offer the interactive PDF instead (`step-execute.md`, "Make it interactive?"), and note "Playable version: later" in their Decisions.
