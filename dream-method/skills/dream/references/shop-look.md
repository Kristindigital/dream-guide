# Their shop's look and image kit

Read this in Apply:
- **Round 1:** once they pick a storefront, tell them which images it needs
- **Rounds 4 and 5:** make the images
- **Round 2:** set up their shop's look (banner, icon, colors and fonts) while the shop is being set up
- **Round 9:** upload the listing images

Their look comes from the style sheet they made in Execute (fonts, colors with hex codes, page frame). Their shop should feel like the product they're selling.
Sizes were checked in October 2026 where the platform publishes them. Where it doesn't, match the size the upload screen shows, and never guess one as fact.

---

## Shop words vs product words

Two levels, never mixed:
- **The shop speaks for the whole niche:** the name, the title or slogan, the banner tagline, the icon, the About section, the bio and the announcement. It must still fit when they add their second, third and tenth product. For example: "Simple budget printables for busy moms".
- **Each product speaks for itself:** the listing title, the description, the mockups and preview images, and the product's thumbnail. For example: "Budget Planner Printable for Moms, Plan Every Paycheck…".

Before you show any shop-level words, check them: would they still be true if the next product were a lunchbox planner? If not, broaden them.

## Who does what

- **You design every image in Canva,** in their look:
  - mockups and preview images
  - banners and icons
  - thumbnails, covers and checkout headers
  - **If they have a Canva Brand Kit:** ask once if they'd like it used. If yes, use the Canva connector's brand-kit design tools, which apply their brand colors, fonts and logo.
  - **Otherwise:** use their style sheet.
- **Systeme.io, with its connector: you build the pages in their look** (colors, fonts, words), then open each page and fix the colors (`storefronts.md`, Systeme.io).
  - **They put the pictures in,** because the connector can't upload images. In the page editor:
    1. Click the image.
    2. Click the upload (cloud) icon.
    3. Choose their mockup, then **Insert**.
  - **Swap every stock photo** for their real hero mockup, especially photos of people.
- **Every other storefront: they click, and you hand them the exact settings.** No connector can change the design settings on Stan Store, Beacons, Etsy, Payhip, Gumroad or Skool. Send one short message:

  > Here's your shop's look, ready to copy:
  > - **Background:** {light hex}
  > - **Text:** {dark hex}
  > - **Buttons:** {bright hex} (use it only for buttons)
  > - **Headings:** {font} · **Body text:** {font}
  >
  > If {platform} doesn't have {font}, pick the closest one in its list (another {rounded / serif / handwritten} font).

  Follow `sales-pages.md`: a light background, dark text, and one bright button color.

## The image kit for each storefront

**Tell them up front (Round 1, right after they pick):**

> On {platform} you'll need {short list}. Don't worry about making them: I'll design every one in Canva for you, in your look. The shop's own images come while we set up your shop, and the listing photos come from your real pages after that.

| Platform | Images to make | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Etsy** | 8 to 10 listing images (the first is the hero mockup); a shop banner; a shop icon | **Listing images:** landscape 2400 x 1800 (4:3), or square 2000 x 2000. Etsy leans toward landscape, wants 2000 px or more, and needs the first photo at least 635 px. Keep the product centered with space around it, and use one shape for every photo. **Banner:** 4:1. That's 3360 x 840 in Kristin's Etsy guide; Etsy recommends 1600 x 400, with a minimum of 1200 x 300. Keep words away from the edges, because phones crop it. **Icon (Etsy's "logo"):** 500 x 500, simple, with short text that reads at a tiny size. **Save everything as JPG under about 1 MB.** |
| **Stan Store** | A product thumbnail for their store page; the checkout page banner image; their profile photo. Stan's help lists only these two product image spots and no gallery, so don't make a listing-photo set. **Make the banner work hard** (Kristin, Oct 17): the product mockup, the product name and 2 or 3 short "what's inside" points, readable on a phone. **Description images:** the **Description Body** box has an image button in its toolbar (Kristin confirmed, Oct 17, 2026; Stan's help doesn't mention it). Offer 1 or 2 graphics to add there, in their look: a "What's inside" preview and a "How it works in 3 steps". Tell them where they go: in the Description Body, click the image button and upload it at that spot in the words. **These images (and the banner) aren't clickable,** so never put anything in them that looks like a button ("Buy now", "Get it here"), an arrow saying "click", or a price (it goes out of date). Stan's real buttons carry the action. If their screen has no image button, keep the words broken up with headings and bullets. | **Thumbnail:** 400 x 400. **Checkout banner:** 1920 x 1080 (Stan's help, October 2026). **Description graphics:** 1920 x 1080 too, so they match the banner (our choice; Stan doesn't list a size). Profile photo: a clear square photo of them. |
| **Beacons** | A product thumbnail plus extra product images; their profile photo | Match the upload screen. A 1080 x 1080 or 2000 x 2000 square is a safe start. |
| **Systeme.io** | The sales page hero mockup; a "what's inside" image if it helps; the product image on the order form | A landscape image around 1600 x 900 fits the page width well. Keep the file small, because big images slow the page. |
| **Payhip** | Product images (the first is the hero) | The aspect ratio set in the store builder. A 2000 x 2000 square is a safe default. |
| **Gumroad** | One or more cover images; a thumbnail | **Cover:** 1280 x 720. **Thumbnail:** at least 600 x 600 (Gumroad's help). |
| **Skool** | The community icon; a cover/thumbnail; course covers; 2 or 3 About page graphics | Match the upload screen (`skool.md`, Steps 1, 4 and 7). |

### Stan Store: a designed checkout page (optional; Kristin, Oct 17, 2026)

Some students want their Stan checkout page to look like a designed landing page, not just text. Offer the choice once, in Apply Round 5, which is called **Checkout page** on a Stan roadmap (Stan has no image gallery, so this is its version of preview images), and give your pick (Kristin, Oct 17):
> Your checkout page can look one of three ways:
> (a) **All text:** the words we just wrote. Simple, quick and easy to change.
> (b) **All images:** a designed landing page, built in your Canva as images stacked in your Description Body. Your must-know details still go below as text.
> (c) **Images and text together:** designed image sections with short real text between them.
>
> ⭐ **My pick: (c).** It looks designed and polished, you can update one section without redoing the page, and your key details (what you get, nothing shipped, refunds) stay as real text buyers can always find.
> Which one would you like?

Then build what they chose. (a) needs nothing more than Round 3's words. For (b) and (c), follow the steps below.

- **4 or 5 stacked section images by default.** Kristin's Stan test (Oct 17) confirmed stacked images show one after another on phone and desktop. Sections beat one long image because one part can be swapped without redoing the page, the files are smaller, and real text can sit between them. Text size is the same either way: width decides it. **How Stan shows them** (measured on Kristin's test page, Oct 17): each image sits a little in from the page edge, with slightly rounded corners and a small white gap before the next one. On a phone each section shows about 343 pixels wide, and the body text in a 1080-wide image was still easy to read. On a computer each section is about 660 wide. If they'd rather have **one long image** (one upload, and no gaps between sections), build that instead, at 1080 wide. Each section is **1080 x 1350** (about one phone screen; our choice, Stan lists no size), in their colors and fonts, with no frame (see the next point), so together they read as one page:
  1. **Hero:** the product mockup, the product name and the promise. **On Stan, the checkout banner sits right above Description Body,** so if the banner already shows the same mockup and headline, leave the hero out and start with the problem. Or make the two different (Kristin's test, Oct 17).
  2. **The problem,** in their buyer's own words
  3. **What's inside:** real pages only
  4. **How it works:** 3 steps
  5. **Who it's for, and who it isn't**
  - **No "What to expect" image.** Those facts (instant download, nothing shipped, refunds, personal use) go at the end as real text, in the FAQ from Round 10, so an image of them would only repeat it (Kristin's test, Oct 17).
- **Design it like a website sales page, not like their printable** (Kristin, Oct 17). Their style sheet gives the colors and fonts, but not the printable's page frame:
  - **No page frame, no border, no rounded corners of your own.** Stan already rounds the corners a little.
  - **Full-width color bands:** switch each section's background between colors from their palette (for example cream, a soft tint of their main color, white), the way a website changes bands. Fill the whole image edge to edge. Stan's small white gaps make each tinted section look like a soft card on a white page, and a white section blends straight into the page. Both look clean. Tell them about the gaps before they upload, so they don't think something went wrong.
  - **Website layout:**
    - a big headline and a short subheadline
    - real mockups of their pages (on a desk or tablet, or laid flat)
    - a row of 3 or 4 icons with short benefits
    - "what's inside" as a grid of page thumbnails with labels
    - numbered steps
    - a ticked checklist
  - **The same side margins on every section,** so everything lines up like one page.
- **Use the words approved in Round 3.** Don't add new claims, and keep each section short, with big text. Check every section at phone size before you save.
- **Keep the must-know words as real text too,** below the images: what they get, the file type, "nothing will be shipped", the refund line and the personal-use line. Words inside images can't be searched, copied or read by screen readers.
- **For (c), the words between images add, never repeat** (Kristin's test, Oct 17). Each image already says its own words, so don't type the same list or steps out again below it, or buyers read every section twice. Between images, use only what the images don't say: the short story (the moment they know, one line on what the product does with their main keyword, and why they made it, which builds trust for a new seller), or a one-line lead-in. Then the must-know words go at the end.
- **No fake buttons, no prices, nothing that isn't inside** (see the Stan Store row above). Stan's real buttons carry the action.
- **One Canva design, one page per section,** with no spare cover and no leftover blank page. If the store card and banner are in the same design, put them after the sections, and give the page numbers when they download. Show a preview, ask, then save, and give them the Canva link. Then tell them how to add it: in **Description Body**, click the image button and upload section 1, then 2, and so on, in order.

## How you make them

1. **Make the main set first** (Rounds 4 and 5), as one Canva design:
   - the hero mockup as a sneak peek
   - then the other mockups and the preview images
2. **Make each storefront's sizes from that set.**
   - **Canva Pro:** use Canva's resize. The Canva connector can resize a design to new dimensions.
   - **Canva Free:** resize is a Pro feature (Canva's help, October 2026). Create each size as its own design instead, with the same look, words and real pages. Their notes from Execute say which plan they have; if not, ask once.
   - After resizing, check nothing important is cut off.
   - For Etsy's banner, keep the words in the middle.
3. **Name each file with their main keyword,** like `budget-planner-printable-hero.jpg`. Write alt text where the platform asks for it (`why-people-buy.md`, section 15).
4. **After saving, always give them the Canva link to the design** and its name, so they can open it in one click.
   - Make sure it holds only the images they need. If a copied or leftover page is in there, ask once, then delete it yourself with the Canva connector's merge-designs tool (delete_pages). Deleting pages needs their OK.
   - Never tell them to skip a page.
5. **They download:** Canva **Share** → **Download** → **JPG**. Use PNG only when they need a transparent background, and never on Etsy.
6. **They upload each one where it goes,** in Round 9. Hand them a short list:
   - **Hero mockup** → the first listing photo / the thumbnail
   - **Banner** → {where}

## Keep it honest

- **Show real pages only.** A mockup scene can be made up (a desk, a fridge, a tablet), but the pages on it must be their real product.
- **No strangers' faces next to the sales words.** Only their own face, or a real customer's with permission.
- **On Etsy,** one preview image says "Digital download, nothing will be shipped".
