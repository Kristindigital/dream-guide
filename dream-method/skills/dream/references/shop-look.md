# Their shop's look and image kit

Read this in Apply:
- **Round 1:** once they pick a storefront, tell them which images it needs
- **Rounds 4 and 5:** make the images
- **Round 2:** set up their shop's look (banner, icon, colors and fonts) while the shop is being set up
- **Round 9:** upload the listing images

Their look comes from the style sheet they made in Execute (fonts, colors with hex codes, page frame). Their shop should feel like the product they're selling.
Sizes were checked in October 2026 where the platform publishes them. Where it doesn't, match the size the upload screen shows, and never guess one as fact.

---

## Shop words vs product words

Two levels, never mixed:
- **The shop speaks for the whole niche:** the name, the title or slogan, the banner tagline, the icon, the About section, the bio and the announcement. It must still fit when they add their second, third and tenth product. For example: "Picky eater printables for moms of school-age kids".
- **Each product speaks for itself:** the listing title, the description, the mockups and preview images, and the product's thumbnail. For example: "Picky Eater Meal Planner for School-Age Kids, One Family Dinner…".

Before you show any shop-level words, check them: would they still be true if the next product were a lunchbox planner? If not, broaden them.

## Who does what

- **You design every image in Canva,** in their look:
  - mockups and preview images
  - banners and icons
  - thumbnails, covers and checkout headers
  - **If they have a Canva Brand Kit:** ask once if they'd like it used. If yes, use the Canva connector's brand-kit design tools, which apply their brand colors, fonts and logo.
  - **Otherwise:** use their style sheet.
- **Systeme.io, with its connector: you build the pages in their look** (colors, fonts, words), then open each page and fix the colors (`storefronts.md`, Systeme.io).
  - **They put the pictures in,** because the connector can't upload images. In the page editor:
    1. Click the image.
    2. Click the upload (cloud) icon.
    3. Choose their mockup, then **Insert**.
  - **Swap every stock photo** for their real hero mockup, especially photos of people.
- **Every other storefront: they click, and you hand them the exact settings.** No connector can change the design settings on Stan Store, Beacons, Etsy, Payhip, Gumroad or Skool. Send one short message:

  > Here's your shop's look, ready to copy:
  > - **Background:** {light hex}
  > - **Text:** {dark hex}
  > - **Buttons:** {bright hex} (use it only for buttons)
  > - **Headings:** {font} · **Body text:** {font}
  >
  > If {platform} doesn't have {font}, pick the closest one in its list (another {rounded / serif / handwritten} font).

  Follow `sales-pages.md`: a light background, dark text, and one bright button color.

## The image kit for each storefront

**Tell them up front (Round 1, right after they pick):**

> On {platform} you'll need {short list}. Don't worry about making them: I'll design every one in Canva for you, in your look. The shop's own images come while we set up your shop, and the listing photos come from your real pages after that.

| Platform | Images to make | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Etsy** | 8 to 10 listing images (the first is the hero mockup); a shop banner; a shop icon | **Listing images:** landscape 2400 x 1800 (4:3), or square 2000 x 2000. Etsy leans toward landscape, wants 2000 px or more, and needs the first photo at least 635 px. Keep the product centered with space around it, and use one shape for every photo. **Banner:** 4:1. That's 3360 x 840 in Kristin's Etsy guide; Etsy recommends 1600 x 400, with a minimum of 1200 x 300. Keep words away from the edges, because phones crop it. **Icon (Etsy's "logo"):** 500 x 500, simple, with short text that reads at a tiny size. **Save everything as JPG under about 1 MB.** |
| **Stan Store** | A product thumbnail for their store page; the checkout page banner image; their profile photo. **That's all:** Stan's help lists only these two product image spots and no gallery, so don't make a listing-photo set. | **Thumbnail:** 400 x 400. **Checkout banner:** 1920 x 1080 (Stan's help, October 2026). Profile photo: a clear square photo of them. |
| **Beacons** | A product thumbnail plus extra product images; their profile photo | Match the upload screen. A 1080 x 1080 or 2000 x 2000 square is a safe start. |
| **Systeme.io** | The sales page hero mockup; a "what's inside" image if it helps; the product image on the order form | A landscape image around 1600 x 900 fits the page width well. Keep the file small, because big images slow the page. |
| **Payhip** | Product images (the first is the hero) | The aspect ratio set in the store builder. A 2000 x 2000 square is a safe default. |
| **Gumroad** | One or more cover images; a thumbnail | **Cover:** 1280 x 720. **Thumbnail:** at least 600 x 600 (Gumroad's help). |
| **Skool** | The community icon; a cover/thumbnail; course covers; 2 or 3 About page graphics | Match the upload screen (`skool.md`, Steps 1, 4 and 7). |

Pins and social posts come later, in Market.

## How you make them

1. **Make the main set first** (Rounds 4 and 5), as one Canva design:
   - the hero mockup as a sneak peek
   - then the other mockups and the preview images
2. **Make each storefront's sizes from that set.**
   - **Canva Pro:** use Canva's resize. The Canva connector can resize a design to new dimensions.
   - **Canva Free:** resize is a Pro feature (Canva's help, October 2026). Create each size as its own design instead, with the same look, words and real pages. Their notes from Execute say which plan they have; if not, ask once.
   - After resizing, check nothing important is cut off.
   - For Etsy's banner, keep the words in the middle.
3. **Name each file with their main keyword,** like `picky-eater-meal-planner-hero.jpg`. Write alt text where the platform asks for it (`why-people-buy.md`, section 15).
4. **After saving, always give them the Canva link to the design** and its name, so they can open it in one click.
   - Make sure it holds only the images they need. If a copied or leftover page is in there, ask once, then delete it yourself with the Canva connector's merge-designs tool (delete_pages). Deleting pages needs their OK.
   - Never tell them to skip a page.
5. **They download:** Canva **Share** → **Download** → **JPG**. Use PNG only when they need a transparent background, and never on Etsy.
6. **They upload each one where it goes,** in Round 9. Hand them a short list:
   - **Hero mockup** → the first listing photo / the thumbnail
   - **Banner** → {where}

## Keep it honest

- **Show real pages only.** A mockup scene can be made up (a desk, a fridge, a tablet), but the pages on it must be their real product.
- **No strangers' faces next to the sales words.** Only their own face, or a real customer's with permission.
- **On Etsy,** one preview image says "Digital download, nothing will be shipped".
