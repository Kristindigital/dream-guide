# Page checks (audit a page like a buyer would)

Read this whenever they ask you to check, review or audit a page a buyer sees ("check my listing", "why isn't this selling?", "look at my sales page"), in Market Round 3 (get found), and in check-ins when the numbers point to a message or offer problem (`keep-growing.md`).
The goal: *the 3 fixes that will help most, ready to use.* Never a 30-point report card.

**What you can check here:** an Etsy listing, their Etsy shop page, a sales or product page (Systeme.io, Stan Store, Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad), their storefront home page, a freebie sign-up page and its thank-you page, their Pinterest profile and pins, and an email. Their Skool About page: use `skool.md`.
**Instagram, Facebook or YouTube profiles** belong to Kristin's social media add-on. If it's available in this chat (`dream-content`), hand off. If not, follow `step-market.md`, "If they want social media".

---

## 1. See the page for real

Check only what you can actually see. Never fill a gap from memory or from what the page "probably" says (`rules.md`, rule 1).

1. **Use their notes first.** They already hold the approved title, description, tags, price and links from Apply. Ask only for what's live and might differ.
2. **Ask for the link and open it yourself.** Then look at what you actually got:
   - **The page opened with its words:** check it.
   - **Little or no text came back** (some page builders load their words with scripts): don't guess. Ask for screenshots.
   - **Etsy usually blocks automated visits.** If it won't open, say so in one line and ask for screenshots. Don't retry.
   - **If the Apify connector is on in this chat** (some students connect it for the social media add-on), its official web browser tool (`apify/rag-web-browser`) can sometimes open a page that blocked you. It uses a little of their Apify credit, so ask first.
3. **Screenshots: ask for everything in one message, at most 4,** taken on their **phone** (most buyers shop on a phone), from the top of the page down. Tell them how in one line for their device (iPhone: the side button and volume up together; Android: power and volume down).
   - **Etsy listing:** (1) the top of the listing, (2) after one scroll, (3) the start of the description. Plus a 4th if they're up for it: search Etsy for their main keyword and screenshot the results where their listing shows, or the first row if it doesn't.
   - **Words are easier pasted than photographed.** For tags (buyers can't see them), ask them to copy them from Shop Manager → **Listings** → their listing → **Tags**.
   - **Shop Manager screens:** ask them to cover buyer names, order numbers and emails first.

## 2. The check

Start every page with the **5-second check** (`step-market.md`, Round 3): as a stranger on a phone, can you tell in 5 seconds **who** it's for, **what** it helps them do, **what's included**, **how** they get it, and **why** it's worth it?
Then go through the list for that page. These are the same rules the guide used to build it, so read the files they point to.

### An Etsy listing
- **The first image:** makes sense as a tiny thumbnail in search, shows the real product, and any text on it is readable on a phone. JPG or a solid background (Etsy shows transparent PNGs badly).
- **The title** (`step-apply.md`, Round 3): main keyword first, in natural words; what it is, who it's for, what it helps with. Up to 140 characters, ideally under 15 words, no "perfect" or "best".
- **The images** (Rounds 4 and 5): about 8 to 10 strong ones: mockups, 2 or 3 real pages big enough to read, the most useful page up close, "Digital download, nothing will be shipped" and "Instructions included". No weak filler.
- **The description:** the main keyword and the buyer's words in the first lines, then Kristin's order: the problem → what staying stuck costs → the product as the bridge → the result → what's included. Then "This is for you if...", what you can expect, what they get and how, what they need, usage, the AI note if AI helped, and the kind no-refund line.
- **Tags and attributes:** all 13 tags used, from their keyword set, multi-word where possible (up to 20 characters each). Every attribute that applies is filled in.
- **The price** matches what they approved. A sale only with a true former price.
- **Trust:** nothing invented ("bestseller", review counts, results). Real reviews only.
- **Marketing settings** (`market-places.md`, section 1): Share & Save enrolled, Offsite Ads decided on purpose.
- **Delivery:** the file is attached, and **Preview** shows what a buyer gets.

### Their Etsy shop page
- **Their look** (`shop-look.md`): the shop icon, banner and listing thumbnails look like one shop.
- **Shop title and announcement:** about their whole niche, with a keyword, never one product.
- **About section:** a real person, what the shop helps with, what buyers can expect. Nothing invented.
- **Policies and FAQ** filled in (`step-apply.md`, Round 10). Sections in place once there are several products, with the best seller first.
- **The same name and look** as their Pinterest and emails.

### A sales page or product page
Use `sales-pages.md` as the checklist, phone first:
- **The top, before scrolling:** a call-out line naming who it's for; a result headline of 3 lines or fewer; a subheadline with the main keyword; one real product picture; the button visible without scrolling (on a page they built).
- **Then, top to bottom:** why trust them (real only), the problem in the buyer's words, how it works, what's inside, for you if / not for you if, the price said plainly, what to expect, FAQ, and the last button.
- **One action only:** the same button words every time, and no links leading away.
- **Design:** no strangers' faces, dark text on light for long copy, a bright button color used nowhere else, nothing cut off on a phone, a page that loads fast.
- **Storefronts** (Stan, Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad): plain text only, no button markers or links in the description, and every box used the way `sales-pages.md` ("Platform notes") says.
- **Systeme.io:** the SEO title and description are filled in. The order form and thank-you page follow "The order form and thank-you page".

### Their storefront home page (Stan Store, Beacons, a Payhip or Gumroad shop)
- The bio says who they help, in their buyer's words (their niche, never one product).
- Their best product is first. Every link works and goes where it says.
- Their photo or logo and colors match their look.

### A freebie sign-up page and thank-you page
- **Short:** what they get, a picture of it, name and email, one button (`market-places.md`, section 3).
- **The same buyer** as the paid product, and the freebie leads naturally to it.
- **It arrives by email,** not as a download on the page. Ask them to sign up with a second email address to check.
- **The thank-you page** gives one next step, and points to the paid product.

### Pinterest
- **Profile:** their name or shop name with their niche keyword, a one-line about with who they help, and their shop link.
- **Boards:** named with phrases their buyer searches, each with a one-line description.
- **Pins** (`market-places.md`, section 2): 2:3 (1000 × 1500 px), a few big words readable on a phone, a title under 100 characters with the main keyword, a 150 to 300 character description ending with what to do next, and a link to the listing (Share & Save on Etsy).

### An email
Ask them to paste it, with the subject line. Check it against "Rules for every email" (`market-places.md`, section 3): under 300 words, a subject under 40 characters, preview text under 30, one purpose, at most one "$", and a clear P.S.

## 3. The report

Use the three labels (`rules.md`, rule 2): what you found, what it means, and what you'd change. Keep it short:

> **Your {listing / page} check**
> **Working well:** {2 or 3 specific, real things}
> **Fix first:**
> 1. **{the biggest fix}:** {what you see} → {why it matters to a buyer} → {the fix}
> 2. ...
> 3. ...
> **Later:** {smaller things, in one line, or "nothing else"}
>
> Want me to {make fix 1: write the new title, redo the first image in Canva...}? Say **yes**.

- **Order the fixes by how much they help someone buy.** Usually in this order: what's missing or broken (a dead link, a missing file), then what stops the 5-second check, then everything else.
- **Write the fix, don't just name it.** The new title, the new first lines, the image built in their Canva. They paste it in and click save themselves.
- **Approved work is protected.** You suggest; they choose.
- **No promises.** Never say a fix will bring a number of sales, or make Etsy or Pinterest show it more, unless the platform's own help says so (link it).
- **In a check-in, change one thing at a time,** so they can tell what helped (`keep-growing.md`). Outside a check-in, they can do all 3.
- **If it passes, say so** in one line, and get back to their plan.

Save it in their notes, under **Page checks**: the date, the page, the 3 fixes, and what they changed.

## Another seller's page (to learn from)

If they'd like to see why a competitor's listing sells, check 1 or 2 pages from their Research (the products that already sell), the same way. Look for the **pattern** (how the first image shows the product, how the title is built, what the description answers early), then use it in their own words and look.
Never copy another seller's words, images or design, and never contact or criticize them. Keep it to the pages, never the people.
