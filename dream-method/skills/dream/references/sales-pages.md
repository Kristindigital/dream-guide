# Sales pages and product pages

Read this in Apply (Rounds 3 and 9) whenever you write or lay out a page a buyer lands on:
- a Stan Store or Beacons product and checkout page
- a Systeme.io sales page, order form or thank-you page
- a Payhip or Gumroad product page
- a Skool About page (with `skool.md`, Step 7)
- later in Market, a freebie (opt-in) page (`upsells-and-funnels.md`, part 2)

It's Kristin's sales page method, adapted for a beginner's first $5 to $50 product.
An Etsy listing has no page to lay out. Use Round 3's description structure there, plus the word rules in `why-people-buy.md`, sections 14 and 15.

**Two kinds of page:**
- **A page you build** (a Systeme.io sales page, a freebie page): you place the buttons, so use every button rule below.
- **A storefront product page** (Stan, Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad): the platform puts its own buy button on the page, and the description is a plain text box.
  - Write the same order below, as plain text.
  - **Never write button markers like "[Get my meal planner]" or links in the description.** Stan's description can't hold clickable buttons or links (Kristin, October 2026).
  - The platform's own button is the only button. Give it the benefit words where it lets you name it (Stan: the thumbnail button and the Call-to-Action Button text).

## First: offer, then words, then page

Kristin's order: **a clear offer → map the copy → build the page.**
The offer (who it's for, the problem, the promise, what's inside, the price) comes from their notes. Write the words before anyone opens a page builder. A pretty page can't fix a fuzzy offer.

## The top of the page (what they see before scrolling, on a phone)

Most visitors come from social media on their phone, so build for the phone first. Kristin's findings put it at 50 to 70% of traffic. The top must answer "is this for me, and what do I get?" in a few seconds:

1. **A call-out line** above the headline, naming who it's for. For example: "For moms whose money is gone before payday".
   It's a banner that qualifies the right buyer. Never a fake urgency banner, and a deadline only if it's true.
2. **The headline: the result, short and specific.**
   - At most 3 lines on a phone and 2 on a computer, so about 4 to 10 words.
   - It takes away their worst problem, in words they can picture. Write "Every bill planned before payday", not "Better money habits".
   - Use a number only if it's true (12 months, 10 pages, 15 minutes).
3. **A subheadline** that says how, or how fast, and carries their **main keyword** (`why-people-buy.md`, section 15).
   For example: "The budget planner printable for busy moms. Plan every paycheck in 15 minutes, with a bill calendar and a spending tracker."
4. **One picture of the real product:** the hero mockup from Apply Round 4.
   If they film a short video instead, they should start full frame, looking at the camera, not as a tiny bubble in the corner of a screen recording. People need to see their face to trust them.
5. **The button,** visible without scrolling on a phone (on a page you build). On a storefront, the platform places its button; keep the description tight so it isn't buried.

**Shorten the headline with Kristin's half-words exercise:**
1. Rewrite it in half the words.
2. Halve it again, until only the must-have words are left.
3. Add back only the words that truly help.

## Then, top to bottom

On a page you build, put the button after every major section, with **the same words every time** ("Get my budget planner"). On a storefront, skip this: its own button is the only one. Either way, one action only: no links to other pages, socials or products.

1. **Why trust them,** right under the top. Use their real credibility in one or two lines: their story, their own result, a real number. ("I'm a mom of two who used to run out of money before every payday.")
   - Real buyer quotes, only with permission, once they exist.
   - Never invented testimonials, results or numbers (`rules.md`, rule 3). Most page templates come with testimonial slots. Delete them rather than fill them.
2. **The problem, in the buyer's own words.** Paint one real moment from their day, like a scene in a movie: "It's the 22nd. You check your bank app in the grocery line, and you're already counting the days to payday." Then name the honest cost of staying stuck, once (`why-people-buy.md`, section 10).
3. **How it works:** the product as the bridge, in 3 simple steps or 3 cards.
4. **What's inside:** benefit first, one line per part, the most wanted part first.
5. **This is for you if... / This isn't for you if...** (`why-people-buy.md`, section 11).
6. **Price:**
   - Say the price plainly, and what's included.
   - A crossed-out "was" price only if they really sold it at that price.
   - No inflated "value" totals.
7. **What to expect:** how they get it (instant download, the link also comes by email), the kind no-refund line, and where to get help if a file won't open.
8. **Questions (FAQ):** 3 or 4 real worries, about fit, time, format and printing, answered in one or two lines each.
9. **The last button,** with a headline that repeats the result ("Every bill, planned."). On Stan, that headline goes in the **Bottom Button Title**, just above Stan's own button.

## Design that sells

- **Simple, not crowded.** Plenty of space, short paragraphs, few images. Every image shows the real product or the real result.
- **No strangers' faces.** Page builders often drop stock photos of people into cards and sections. Remove any face that isn't the seller's own, or a real customer's shared with permission: next to the copy it reads like a testimonial.
- **Dark text on a light background.** In Kristin's findings, people read it about 40% faster than light text on dark. Dark sections are fine for a short band, not for long copy.
- **Buttons are bright and high contrast,** in a color that appears nowhere else on the page. In Kristin's findings, red, yellow and green perform best; pick whichever stands out against their brand colors. The button doesn't have to be pretty. It has to be seen.
- **Check gradients and dark backgrounds on a phone.** A gradient that looks fine on a computer can hide the headline on a phone.
- **Fast pages sell more.** Use JPGs or compressed PNGs at the size the page needs, not huge originals. If a page loads slowly, the images are usually why.
- **Check the phone view before calling it done.** Most builders have a phone preview (Systeme.io, Stan, Beacons and Payhip all do). Headline under 3 lines, nothing cut off, and (on a page you build) the button visible without scrolling.

## The order form and thank-you page (if their platform has them)

- **Order form:** repeat the promise in one line, the price, what they get (3 to 5 lines), then the checkout. No extra buttons that lead away from paying.
- **Thank-you page:** confirm it worked, then give one next step they can take right now (open the product, plus "the link is in your email too"). If they have an add-on or a next product, this is a natural place to mention it (`upsells-and-funnels.md`).

## Before you show it: the read-through

Read it as a stranger on a phone:
- Would I know in 5 seconds who it's for and what I get?
- Is every sentence something I could check or picture? (`why-people-buy.md`, section 14)
- Is it written at about a 4th or 5th grade reading level, in their voice? Read it out loud: it should sound like them talking.
- On a page you build: does every button say the same thing, and do I know what happens when I tap it? On a storefront: is it plain text, with no button markers or links?
- Their main keyword is in the product name, the subheadline and the first lines. Is it there, and does it still read naturally?
