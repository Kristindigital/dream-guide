# Places to market (the setups)

Read the section for the student's main place (`step-market.md`, Round 2). Each one has: what it is, how to set it up, their weekly activity, what to watch, and the honesty rules.
Platform facts below were checked on the platforms' own help pages in October 2026. Screens and fees change: check the current page before you state a fee, a limit or a menu path, and link it (`rules.md`, rule 5).
None of these need social media content or filming. For social media, see `step-market.md`, "If they want social media".

**Everywhere:** use their keyword set and their buyer's words (`why-people-buy.md`, sections 7 and 15), their message bank (Round 4), and their look. Build every graphic in their Canva. They create their own accounts and click their own buttons.

---

## 1. Etsy search

Etsy shoppers arrive already searching. For an Etsy seller, **the listing is the marketing.** Round 3 (get found) is most of the work.

**Set up (once):**
1. **The listing fixes from Round 3:** title, first lines of the description, main image, tags, all in their buyer's words.
2. **Share & Save:** sellers who enroll get **4% of the order total back** on orders placed within 30 days of a buyer clicking a link they shared (to their shop, a listing or a promo code). It counts for links shared off Etsy (Pinterest, email, texts, their website), not inside Etsy messages or listing descriptions. Help them enroll, then use their Share & Save links everywhere they share. Official: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/16981332744087
3. **Offsite Ads (decide, don't ignore):** every shop is enrolled automatically. Etsy advertises their listings on other sites and charges a fee **only** on sales from those ads: **15%** for shops under $10,000 in sales in any 365 days, **12%** above that. Shops under $10,000 can opt out (**Shop Manager → Settings → Offsite Ads**). Once a shop reaches $10,000, it's required from then on. Explain the trade in one line and let them choose. Official: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000338367
4. **Sales and coupons:** optional. A sale must be real (a true former price). A launch price is fine if it's true.

**Their weekly activity:** one small improvement to a listing (one thing at a time), and new listings for the same buyer as they grow (`keep-growing.md`). Each listing is one more way to be found. Pair Etsy with **Pinterest** or **people they know** to send more shoppers in, using Share & Save links.

**Watch:** Etsy's **Stats** in Shop Manager: visits, favorites and orders.

**Rules:**
- **Never add an Etsy buyer to an email list without their clear consent** (Etsy's Seller Policy: https://www.etsy.com/legal/sellers). Don't send buyers off Etsy from listings or Etsy messages. Their freebie and email list live elsewhere (section 3), promoted on Pinterest or in communities.
- **Never trade anything for a review.** Etsy bans rewards for positive reviews; our rule is no rewards at all.
- **Etsy Ads** (paid) are not a first-sale step. See section 9.

## 2. Pinterest

People use Pinterest like a search engine for ideas and plans. A pin links straight to their listing, and in Kristin's experience a good pin can keep sending people to a product for months. It fits printables, planners, templates and guides especially well, and it needs no filming.

**Set up (once):** if they ask for pins first ("Make 5 Pinterest pins for my product"), make the pins. Then, before they post, ask in one line: "Do you already have a Pinterest business account with boards?" If not, walk them through these steps (Kristin's test, Oct 17: the pins came with no account or boards).
1. **A free Pinterest business account** (they sign up themselves at pinterest.com/business). Use the same brand name as their shop.
2. **Profile:** their name or shop name with their niche keyword (for example "{Their name} | Budget Printables for Moms"), and a one-line about with who they help (their niche, never one product). Add their shop link.
3. **2 or 3 boards** named with phrases their buyer searches ("Budget Planner Printables", "Money Tips for Busy Moms"), each with a one-line description using their keywords.

**Their pins** (you design them in Canva, in their look):
- **Size:** 2:3, **1000 × 1500 px**. That's Pinterest's recommendation (https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs). Canva's "Pinterest Pin" design is this size.
- **Five different pins per product to start,** all linking to the same listing:
  1. The main mockup (from Apply)
  2. A peek inside (one real page)
  3. Problem → solution (a line from their message bank)
  4. A list ("5 bills to plan before payday"), with the product as the next step
  5. Before → after (their promise)
- **Text on the pin:** the result in a few big words, plus the main keyword if it fits. Readable on a phone.
- **Pin title:** under 100 characters (Pinterest's limit), with their main keyword. Write 3 options.
- **Pin description:** Kristin's shape: 150 to 300 characters, start with the main benefit, 2 or 3 keywords used naturally, and end by telling them what to do next ("Get the printable planner here.").
- **Link:** the listing. Etsy sellers use their **Share & Save** link.

**Their weekly activity:** a steady number of fresh pins they can keep up (Kristin recommends a simple 30-day pinning plan; 3 to 5 a week is a realistic start). New designs for the same product count. Save each pin to the best-matching board.

**Watch:** Pinterest's analytics: impressions (seen), saves and outbound clicks (interested), and then sales on their shop.

**Seasons and scheduling** (checked October 17, 2026):
- Pinterest's creator advice is to launch seasonal content 4 to 6 weeks before big moments (https://create.pinterest.com/blog/leverage-seasonal-trends-moments/). Its business advice is to be ready a couple of months ahead (https://business.pinterest.com/blog/guide-top-pinterest-marketing-moments-2025/).
- **Pinterest Trends** shows when searches for a keyword rise and fall, so they can see when a season starts (https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-trends).
- Business accounts can schedule a pin up to 30 days ahead, with up to 10 pins scheduled at a time (https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/schedule-pins).

**Rules:** no "bestseller", review counts or results on a pin unless they're true and theirs. Pinterest ads are for later (section 9).

## 3. An email list (your own people)

An email list belongs to them. No app can take it away or stop their messages from reaching people. A small list of the right people is a real start.

**The pieces** (the freebie and its pages: `upsells-and-funnels.md`, part 2, including the platform table):
1. **The freebie:** a small, quick piece of the same problem that leads to the paid product. Suggest 5 ideas they could make in Canva in under an hour (Kristin's way), each attracting the same person who'd buy the product. Build the one they pick in Canva (one to five pages, `execute-blueprints.md` for the shape).
2. **The sign-up page:** short. What they get, a picture of it, name and email, one button.
3. **The delivery email:** the freebie arrives by email, never as a download on the page.
4. **The welcome emails** (automated, one a day, 3 to 5 to start):
   1. **Welcome** (right away): deliver the freebie in the first lines, one simple tip they can use today, and a question to reply to ("What's your biggest {problem} right now?"). Replies tell inboxes the sender is wanted.
   2. **Value** (day 2): one useful thing. No selling.
   3. **Story** (day 3): their true story of the struggle and the turning point, in their words. The product comes in as what changed things. Ask them for the story; never invent it.
   4. **The product** (day 4): who it's for, what's inside (as results, not features), the price and the link. Answer their buyer's biggest doubt.
   5. **Reminder** (day 5): a short, kind reminder. A deadline or bonus only if it's real.
5. **A launch email** (for their list when the product is new): 2 or 3 sentences on the struggle they're in right now → what life looks like once it's gone → the product as the bridge → 3 to 5 things inside, written as results → what to do, with the price and link → a P.S. with a real reason to act now, or none. Write 5 subject line options.

**Rules for every email** (Kristin's findings):
- Under 300 words, at a 4th or 5th grade reading level, in their tone. Read it out loud: if they wouldn't say it, rewrite it.
- **Subject line:** under 40 characters, ideally 4 words or fewer. Natural, lowercase is fine. Avoid "$", "free", "now", "last chance" and "special deal" in the subject.
- **Preview text:** under 30 characters. It adds curiosity; it never answers the subject.
- The first line backs up the subject. One purpose per email. The P.S. is the call to action most of the time.
- **At most one "$" in an email.** Kristin has seen an email go to spam over two.
- Check each email at mailmeteor.com/spam-checker and fix what it flags.

**Consent and deliverability:**
- **Only email people who signed up.** Never buy or borrow a list. Make unsubscribing easy.
- Send from the platform's sender with their own name. Their own domain is best if the platform allows it. Never send through a marketing tool from a personal Gmail.
- After about 90 days with no opens, send one "still want these?" email, then remove the people who don't answer.

**Where:** their storefront's email tools if it has them (Systeme.io, Stan Store, Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad: check the current features and plan limits live). **Etsy sellers:** the list lives on a separate platform with its own sign-up page, never fed by Etsy buyers (section 1).

**Their weekly activity:** one email a week to their list: a tip, a story or the product, with a gentle invitation in the P.S. Plus their way of bringing people to the sign-up page (Pinterest, communities, people they know).

**Watch:** sign-ups, opens, clicks, replies, and sales.

## 4. People you already know (the warm launch)

The fastest first sale usually comes from people who already know them. Kristin's launch order (`upsells-and-funnels.md`): warm first, then colder.

**Set up:**
1. **Their list of 20:** people who'd genuinely use it, or know someone who would. Friends, family, coworkers, other parents, groups they're already part of, past customers who agreed to hear from them.
2. **Their message,** written by you in their tone, short and personal. The shape:
   - hello, and something personal ("How's the new job?")
   - why they're writing ("I just finished something I'm really proud of")
   - why they thought of this person ("You mentioned you've been trying to budget")
   - what it is, in one line, with the link
   - an easy out ("No pressure at all. If you know someone it'd help, I'd love it if you passed it on.")
   A short voice note or a quick screen-share video works too, if they like that. Keep it under 2 minutes.

**Their weekly activity:** a set number of personal messages (for example 10), each one personalized. Never copy-paste the same text to everyone.

**Rules:**
- One message per person, no chasing. Never spam strangers, buy contact lists or mass-tag people.
- Asking friends to share is fine. Asking them for reviews of something they didn't buy and use is not (`rules.md`, rule 3).

## 5. Communities (help first)

Their buyer is already asking questions somewhere: a Facebook group, a subreddit, a forum, a Skool community, the comments of a parenting or hobby site. Research may have found these places (the links behind their buyer's quotes). This is helping one person at a time, not content creation.

**Set up:**
1. **Pick 2 or 3 places** where their buyer asks real questions and that are active.
2. **Read each place's rules** before posting. Many forbid self-promotion, or allow it only in one weekly thread or with permission. Save the rule for each place in their notes.
3. **Join as a member,** with their real name or shop name.

**How to show up:**
- **Help first.** Answer real questions with real help: a tip, a step, an idea from their product's content. No link.
- **Share the product only where the rules allow it,** and say it's theirs: "I made a planner for exactly this. Happy to share the link if it'd help."
- **Use their buyer's words** and their message bank, so it sounds like a real person, because it is.

**Their weekly activity:** a set number of genuinely helpful answers (for example 5), plus the product shared only in allowed spots.

**Never:** message members who didn't ask, post the same thing in many groups, pretend to be a happy customer, or argue with anyone.

**Skool:** the same idea applies in neighboring Skool communities (`skool.md`, posting inside the community).

## 6. Partnerships

Other people already have their buyer's attention. Think *a gym and a supplement shop*: same customers, not competitors.

**Ideas** (pick 1 or 2):
- **A shout-out swap:** each mentions the other's product or freebie to their list or group.
- **A bundle** with a seller whose product fits theirs (a meal planner plus someone's grocery list).
- **A guest spot:** a tip in someone's newsletter, a short talk in someone's community, a podcast interview, a free mini-workshop in a group.
- **Affiliates:** if their platform has an affiliate feature, others can share their product for a share of each sale. Check the platform's current page first.
- **Local:** libraries, schools, parent groups, coaches or shops that serve the same person, where it truly fits.

**Set up:** a list of 5 people or businesses that serve the same buyer, and a short first message from you: specific, about how it helps *their* audience, with one small ask.
**Their weekly activity:** 2 or 3 outreach messages.
**Rules:** paid or affiliate relationships are disclosed clearly. No pressure, and no follow-ups after a clear no.

## 7. Google (a long game)

A simple page or blog post that answers one question their buyer searches ("how to make a family budget"), with the product as the next step. Their page settings (SEO title and description, web address) come from Apply.
It can take months to be found, so it's rarely the main place for a first sale. Offer it as a later second place for someone who likes writing.

## 8. Skool

For students who sell on Skool, their community is the place: Discovery (a description and About page in their buyer's words), posts inside the community, live calls if they host them, and neighboring communities. Everything is in `skool.md`.

## 9. Paid ads (later, not now)

Etsy Ads, Pinterest ads and similar are not a first-sale step. Paid ads make sense only once their listing already sells without them, so they know it converts.
If they ever try ads: a small budget they're truly fine losing, a short test, and a check of the results. Never promise that ads will pay for themselves.

## 10. More marketplaces (where buyers already shop)

Other big marketplaces where buyers are already searching. Each one puts their product, or a new version of it, in front of a new crowd.
**Usually a second place,** added once their main place is set up, unless one is clearly the best fit for their buyer (a classroom resource and Teachers Pay Teachers, for example). Recommend only the ones that fit their product, never the whole list.
Facts below were checked on each platform's own help pages in October 2026. Fees and rules change: check the live page before you state one, and link it (`rules.md`, rule 5).

### Amazon KDP (a printed version on Amazon)

**Fits:** planners, journals, logbooks, notebooks and workbooks, anything that works as a printed book. Buyers find it in Amazon search, and Amazon prints and ships each copy when it's ordered.
**What it takes:**
- A new version of their product, built in their Canva: a print-ready interior PDF (paperbacks need at least 24 pages) and a full cover (front, spine and back). Take the trim size, bleed and cover size from KDP's own help pages and cover tools for their page count. Never guess a size.
- Their own free KDP account, with their tax information and bank details. They do those clicks.

**Rules that matter:**
- **Low-content books** (notebooks, planners, journals, log books) don't need an ISBN, and can't use KDP's free ISBN. They must tick the **Low-content** box under Categories, or KDP rejects the book. Low-content books get no Expanded Distribution and can't be a series, and without an ISBN the "look inside" sample isn't shown. KDP says puzzle and coloring books generally aren't low-content.
- **Say so if AI made it.** KDP asks whether any text, images or translations were AI-generated, meaning an AI tool created them, even if they edited them afterward. Content they made themselves and polished with AI is "AI-assisted" and doesn't need disclosing. Help them answer honestly.
- Keep text and images out of the barcode area on the back cover.

**Money:** on Amazon.com, the royalty is 60% of the list price at $9.99 and up (50% at $9.98 and below), minus the printing cost. Run their page count through KDP's Printing Cost & Royalty Calculator before they set a price.
Official: low-content books https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GGE5T76TWKA85DJM · royalties https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330 · content guidelines https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390

### Printify (their designs on real products)

**Fits:** designs that work on shirts, mugs, tote bags, posters or stickers, like a phrase their buyer loves or the art from their product. It's a new, physical product line, so it's best once their first product is selling.
**How it works:** they put their design (made in their Canva, saved as a high-quality PNG) on Printify's products. Printify connects to their shop (Etsy, Shopify, TikTok Shop in the US, eBay, Wix, Squarespace and more, or a free Printify Pop-up store), and a print partner makes and ships each item only when someone orders it. Printify charges them the product and shipping cost for each order, and they keep the difference.
**Costs:** the Free plan is $0 a month, for up to 5 stores. Premium ($39 a month, or $24.99 a month billed yearly) adds product discounts, so it's only worth it once orders are steady. Price each item so it still earns after the product cost, shipping and their shop's fees. Suggest they order one sample before listing, so they've seen what their buyer will get.
**Etsy rules:** only their own original designs (or a buyer's personalization). They must name Printify as a production partner on the listing and in their shop's About section, and give the real ship-from location. Never sell designs they didn't create.
Printful works the same way, if they prefer it. Etsy lists both as print-on-demand partners.
Official: https://printify.com/pricing/ · https://printify.com/integrations/ · Etsy: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336547-Working-with-Production-Partners-on-Etsy

### Teachers Pay Teachers (TPT)

**Fits:** teachers and homeschool sellers making classroom resources. Teachers already search TPT for exactly this, so for a classroom product it can be their main place.
**Costs:**
- **Basic Seller:** a one-time $29 fee. They keep 55% of each sale, minus 30 cents per resource.
- **Exclusive Seller:** $59.95 a year. They keep 77.5% of each sale (80% until January 1, 2027), and pay 15 cents per resource only on orders under $3.

**The catch with Exclusive:** their digital educational resources must be sold only on TPT. If they also sell those resources on Etsy or their own shop, Basic is the honest fit.
Official: https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044219891-Seller-Fees-and-Payout-Rates

**Selling well on TPT** (TPT's help center, checked October 17, 2026). Use these whenever they sell on TPT:
- **Keywords:** **Keyword Finder** (Seller Dashboard → **TPT Keywords**) shows how often buyers searched a keyword across TPT in the last 30 days, with related keywords and an Opportunity Score (Strong, Promising, Moderate, Challenging, Very Difficult). Basic Sellers get 3 searches a day; Exclusive Sellers get unlimited. TPT says keywords in the title and description help reach buyers who search for them. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/52639259916692-What-is-TPT-s-Keyword-Finder
- **Search Analytics** (Exclusive Sellers): the keywords that brought buyers to their products, with visits, conversions (a purchase within 24 hours) and earnings, back to January 1, 2025. It doesn't show rankings or impressions. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/47576021987348-What-is-the-Search-Analytics-feature-and-how-does-it-work
- **Product Insights Report:** each product's numbers over time, including Wish Listed, Saved for Later, and first-time vs repeat buyers. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/43226030198804-How-is-the-Product-Insights-Report-different-from-the-Seller-Dashboard
- **Linking:** the description editor's link tool links to their other resources. Don't link to or advertise stores outside TPT from TPT, and keep prices consistent if they sell elsewhere. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043773292-How-do-I-link-to-another-resource-in-my-product-description · https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044219551-Guidelines-around-utilizing-other-sites
- **Their buyers:** TPT doesn't show who downloads free products, and unsolicited contact outside TPT counts as spam. The **Note to Followers** is a weekly message to their store's followers (banner 1200 x 150). https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/37924778680852-What-is-a-Note-to-Followers
- **Exclusive Sellers off TPT:** they can promote their resources anywhere, and offer free resources (for example to grow an email list that points to TPT), but paid digital educational resources are sold only on TPT, and they can't charge on TPT for something free elsewhere. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/53904743536276-Exclusive-Seller-FAQs-for-New-Sellers-and-Basic-Sellers
- **Bundles:** a TPT Bundle holds 2 to 500 resources and updates itself when they change. A growing bundle must say clearly, at the top, what's in it now and what comes when. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042191412-What-is-the-TPT-Bundle-feature-and-how-is-it-different-from-traditional-bundles · https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042198892-Can-I-offer-a-growing-bundle-or-progressive-bundle
- **No duplicates:** the same resource can't be posted twice (for another grade or keyword). Bundles are the exception. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042429692-Can-I-post-the-same-resource-more-than-once
- **Sales:** sellers can throw a sale of 5, 10, 15 or 20% off, for one resource or the whole store, and choose whether they join TPT's sitewide sales automatically. TPT announces sitewide sales by email and social media. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042885551-How-do-I-throw-a-sale · https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/51910088960532-When-is-the-next-sitewide-sale
- **Promotion:** paid promotion slots were retired on July 31, 2026. The free tools are Search Analytics, Product Insights, Seller Discounts (for example for followers or abandoned carts) and Collaborative Sales, plus the TPT Newsletter (submit from the **Marketing** tab; mostly single resources, not bundles). https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042429932-Are-promotion-slots-available-for-purchase-on-TPT · https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/35735089698324-How-do-I-submit-a-resource-for-the-TPT-Newsletter
- **Retiring a listing:** inactivate it (it can be edited or reused later) or delete it (permanent). Past buyers can still download it either way. When a file changes, **Notify Buyers** shows past buyers a note on their My Purchases page. https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042429472-How-do-I-delete-a-product-from-my-store · https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042472592-How-do-I-notify-Buyers-when-I-update-or-revise-a-resource

### Creative Market

**Fits:** Canva templates, graphics and fonts for other creators and small businesses.
**Costs:** they apply to open a shop. By default they earn 50% of the list price on each sale (rates can vary by shop or product), and Creative Market covers the card fees.
Official: https://support.creativemarket.com/hc/en-us/articles/201193714-All-About-Shop-Sales-Analytics

### Canva Creators (a different way to earn, later)

Not a shop: Canva pays royalties when people use their templates inside Canva. Template Creators apply with a portfolio. The program is in beta, and an answer can take a couple of months. Teachers can apply as Education Specialty Creators.
Bring it up only if they love making templates and ask about more ways to earn. Before they put a design in Canva's library, have them check Canva's Creator terms about selling the same design elsewhere.
Official: https://www.canva.com/help/canva-creators-program/
