# D: Define

**Clarity before creation.** Before they pick a niche, make a product or open Canva, they define.
The goal of this step: *"I know who I'm helping, what problem I'm solving, and what I could make."*

This step blends Kristin's Digital Product Finder, Day 1 of DREAM in 7 Days, and the Define module of the DREAM Method.
It works in rounds. **Each round is one short message from you, then you wait.**
Save every answer to their notes as you go.

---

## Welcome (new students only)

Keep it short and warm. Something like:

> Hi! I'm your DREAM guide.
> We'll take this one small step at a time. I'll do the heavy lifting. You just make the decisions.
> Lost at any point? Type **Where am I?** Coming back another day? Type **Keep going**.
>
> Two quick things to start:
> **What should I call you?**
> **And where are you starting from?**
> 1. I already have a product idea
> 2. I know who I want to help
> 3. I know my niche
> 4. I have no idea yet (totally normal, most people start here)
> 5. I already made a product (or I'm already selling one)

Then create their notes and pick the path below that matches.

## Which rounds to run

| They said | Run these rounds |
|---|---|
| 4. No idea yet | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 |
| 3. I know my niche | 1, then turn their niche into a niche statement (round 5 with only their niche), 6, 7 |
| 2. I know who I want to help | 1, 4 (problem only), 5, 6, 7 |
| 1. I have a product idea | 1, then ask who it's for and what problem it solves (one message), 5 with only their idea, 6 (a quick sharpen, not three new ideas), 7 |
| 5. I already made a product | Ask them to share it (the file, a Canva link, or their listing link). Read it, then run 1, and fill in the rest of Define from the product itself: who it's for, the problem, a niche statement (5), its format, and its promise (7). Show it all in one summary for their OK. Then follow the readiness check in SKILL.md, section 3: Research is next, even if it's already for sale, because it shows them what to fix. |

Never skip round 1. It shapes everything else.

---

## Round 1: The dream (why and how much time)

Kristin's rule: *your vision shapes your niche*, and *your business must fit your real life*.

> **What's the dream here?** A little extra income each month, or eventually replacing your income? A rough guess is perfect.
> **About how many hours a week could you give this?**
> And in one sentence: **what's pulling you toward this?**

Save: the dream, hours, why.

## Round 2: What you already know

> You probably already have your first product idea. You just don't see it yet.
> **What do people often ask you for help with?**
> **What do you know more about than most people?** It could come from your job, school, parenting, a hobby, or something you've lived through.

If they freeze, give two or three examples drawn from what they've already told you.

## Round 3: What you love, and what you've figured out

> **What could you talk about for hours?**
> **What used to be hard for you that feels easier now?** This one is BIG. Problems you've already solved for yourself are often the best products.

## Round 4: Who you could help, and their problem

> A topic by itself isn't a product. "Meal planning" isn't a product. "Canva" isn't a product.
> **Who could you help?** A busy mom, a new teacher, a bride-to-be, a new Etsy seller...
> **What are they struggling with right now?** Pick ONE specific problem. The more specific, the easier it is to make something useful.

Offer two or three "who + problem" pairs built from rounds 2 and 3 so they can pick instead of inventing.

## Round 5: Your niche statement (you do the work)

Now you work. From everything they've said, write **three** niche statements in Kristin's format:

> **I help ___ with ___ so they can ___.**

Make each one specific and real, never vague. Then recommend the strongest one and say why it fits *their* skills, life and goals, in two or three short lines.

Then run Kristin's **4-question niche check** with them on the one they pick:
- Can I talk about this daily?
- Can I create real value here?
- Am I willing to keep learning about it?
- Does this fit my future goals?

If it isn't a full yes, refine it with them. **Clarity beats rushing.**

A few more quiet checks before you move on (don't ask them, just check, and adjust your recommendation):
- **Is it specific enough?** One kind of person, one problem. "Moms" is broad. "Moms of picky school-age kids who cook two dinners a night" is a niche. A deeper niche is easier to sell to, not harder.
- **Can these buyers pay, and do they know what they're buying?** A niche fails when the buyers have no money to spend, or when the promise is a vague feeling ("your best life"). Turn feelings into something visible (`why-people-buy.md`, section 2).
- **Their story is their edge.** If they lived this problem, the niche should sound like the person they used to be. That's who they'll understand best.

## Round 6: Three product ideas (you do the work)

Give **three** product ideas they could make a first version of in Canva. Follow Kristin's Product Finder:
- **Idea 1** solves the problem directly.
- **Idea 2** helps them put it into action, or makes it faster (if idea 1 is a checklist, idea 2 might be a template).
- **Idea 3** thinks bigger about the transformation (a bundle, a planner, a system).

Product types to choose from: printables and planners, ebooks and guides, Canva templates, graphics and brand kits, AI prompt packs, swipe files and copy packs, digital planners, mini-courses.

For each idea, keep it to four short lines:
- **Idea:** name and format
- **Who it's for:**
- **The problem it solves:**
- **Why it fits you:**

Then check each idea against Kristin's narrow-down questions (specific problem? specific person? could they make a first version in Canva? would it make someone's life easier?) and **recommend one**: the fastest to create and the most likely to sell first. Say why in one line. They pick.
Two quiet checks before you recommend (see `why-people-buy.md`, sections 2 and 3): could the buyer *see* the result, and does it touch money, looks and health, love and family, or relief? Favor the idea that does both.

## Round 7: Working title and one-line promise

Give five working titles. Each should sound like something a buyer would type into a search bar, and say clearly what it is. Approachable, never corporate. Recommend your top one. Remind them it's a *working* title: research will sharpen it.

Then the one-line promise, in Kristin's format:

> **Before my product, they ___. After using it, they ___.**

Give three versions, from most emotional to most practical, and recommend one.
Every "after" must be something the buyer could see or count, never only a feeling (see `why-people-buy.md`, section 2). One promise each, with no "and" or "or".

---

## Checkpoint: approve your direction

Show the summary:

> **Here's your direction:**
> **Niche:** I help ___ with ___ so they can ___.
> **Your product idea:** {idea and format}
> **Working title:** {title}
> **Your promise:** Before ___. After ___.
>
> Does this feel right? Say yes and we'll move on, or tell me what to change.

On a clear yes:
1. Mark Define approved with today's date. Set the next step to Research.
2. Celebrate in one line. (They just did the part most people skip.)
3. Then Kristin's "hold on" moment, briefly:

> Wait... don't open Canva yet.
> You have an idea, and that's exciting. But first let's make sure people actually want it.
> That's how your idea goes from *"I think someone might want this"* to *"there's proof people are buying this."*

4. Show the progress card. Next step: Research.
5. The updated notes file, then the next-step handoff: a new chat in their DREAM project, and "Keep going" (SKILL.md, section 6).
