# A: Apply

**Let's get you ready to sell.** Their product is made. Now it needs a home, the words and pictures that sell it, a fair price, protection, a smooth way to reach buyers, and a shop that looks trustworthy.
You choose, write, design and check. They make the decisions that need a human (where to sell, their OK on words, images and price) and do the clicks only they can do (their accounts, ID checks and bank details).
The goal of this step: *"My product is for sale, and I'm proud of how it looks."*

**Selling isn't one moment, it's the whole journey.** The first image they see, the title, the description, the price, how the file arrives and the thank-you message all decide whether the right person buys and is glad they did. That's why Apply covers every one of them.

This step is Kristin's full Apply method:

| Kristin's Apply method | Round |
|---|---|
| Choose your storefront | 1 (first, because it decides title length, image sizes and delivery options, just like Day 5) |
| Set up the shop (skipped if they already have one) | 2 |
| Package your product: title and description | 3 |
| Package your product: mockups | 4 |
| Package your product: preview images | 5 |
| Price it right, plus an add-on if they have a second product | 6 |
| Protect your product | 7 |
| Deliver your product: public view link, template link, thank-you PDF and bundles, Google Drive | 8 |
| List it, finishing touches, buyer's-eye check | 9, 10, 11 |

It works in rounds. **Each round is one short message from you, then you wait.**

**Read before you start:**
- `storefronts.md`: the platforms, who can get paid where, and each platform's setup steps.
- `why-people-buy.md`: sections 5 and 7 (the promise and its versions), 9 to 12 (the gap, real urgency, who it's for, selling without feeling salesy), 14 (plain, specific words) and 15 (keywords everywhere).
- `sales-pages.md`: for any page a buyer lands on (Stan, Beacons, Systeme.io, Payhip, Gumroad, Skool's About page).
- `upsells-and-funnels.md`: for add-ons, when they have a second product.
- `delivery.md`: how the file reaches the buyer on their platform, step by step (Rounds 8 and 9).
- `buyer-email.md`: the email buyers get after paying, with the template and each platform's setup (Rounds 9 and 11).
- `shop-look.md`: the images each storefront needs and their sizes, and how their brand look goes into the store (Rounds 1, 2, 4, 5 and 9).
- `rules.md` again. Selling words are where honesty slips most easily.

## Before you start: the readiness check

Run the readiness check (SKILL.md, section 3). Apply needs, from their notes or from what they share:
- **From Define:** who it's for, the problem, the niche statement.
- **From Research:** proof people buy it, buyer's words, their keyword set (main keyword, supporting phrases, phrases to avoid), price, product name, promise sentence, country. If they only have a loose list of phrases, boil it down to the keyword set first (`step-research.md`, part 4).
- **From Execute:** the finished product (Canva link or file), what's inside, the look (style sheet).

If they arrived with a product they made elsewhere, read it first. Then fill only what's missing, the quick way.

## Round 1: Choose your storefront

Kristin's rules: *there's no perfect storefront, your country matters, think about your selling style, and your first storefront doesn't have to be forever.*

**Their country comes first.** It decides which platforms can pay them at all. It's in their notes (from Research). If it isn't, ask it before anything else, on its own:
> Quick one first: **which country do you live in?** It decides which shops can pay you.

Then quietly check which platforms can pay them in that country (`storefronts.md`, Step 1), and ask one short group of questions:

> Four quick ones, then I'll pick your storefront:
> 1. **Do you already have a storefront in mind?** (a) Yes: tell me which one (b) No, pick for me
> 2. **How do you want buyers to find you?** (a) They search a marketplace, no social media needed (b) From my social media (c) Not sure yet
> 3. **Your budget to start?** (a) Free (b) Up to about $30 a month (c) Happy to invest more
> 4. **What matters most?** (a) The simplest setup (b) An email list and automation (c) Selling a course or community (d) Building a full brand store over time
>
> Just reply with four letters, like **b, a, a, a** (or name your storefront for number 1).

**If they have a storefront in mind:**
- **It can pay them in their country, and it fits their answers:** go with it. Say why it works in one line.
- **It can pay them, but another fits much better:** say so honestly, once, with the reason (`rules.md`, honest feedback). Then go with their choice if they still want it.
- **It can't pay them in their country:** say it plainly and kindly, with the platform's official link, and recommend the best one that can. Never set them up on a shop that can't pay them.

**If not,** recommend **one** platform and one backup, using `storefronts.md` Step 2. Only ones that can pay them where they live:

> **My pick for you: {platform}.**
> - **Why:** {one line tied to their answers}
> - **Cost:** {from the official page, with the link}
> - **Getting paid in {their country}:** {yes, how: for example, straight to your bank account, or through a free Payoneer account}
> - **Backup:** {platform}, if you'd rather {reason}.
>
> Want to go with {platform}?

**Whenever Systeme.io is your pick or the backup** (Kristin, Oct 17), add this line to the recommendation:
> **Bonus:** Systeme.io connects straight to Claude, so I can build most of your shop with you right inside your account: your sales page, product, price, checkout and emails. You'll still connect your payments and do a few clicks yourself, and I'll walk you through those.

Keep it to that. Never say it builds the whole shop, or that it's done for them (`storefronts.md`, Systeme.io: payments, the product file and a few editor settings stay theirs). It's a reason, not the reason: recommend Systeme.io only when it fits their answers.

- **Re-check the price and their country** on the platform's official page before you state them.
- **Etsy's AI rules come first for two kinds of product** (`storefronts.md`, Etsy): never recommend Etsy for an AI prompt pack (Etsy bans them), and recommend Etsy for a how-to product only if its steps are in their own words (their Decisions from Execute). If they want Etsy anyway, explain the rule kindly, once, with the link, and offer the fix: their own words, or another platform.
- If you share one of Kristin's links, disclose it (`storefronts.md`).
- **Their choice is theirs.** If they prefer another platform that can pay them, go with it.
- **Search and social, both?** Start with one. They can add the second later in Market.

Save the platform, and why, in their notes.
Then tell them, in one or two lines, which images that storefront needs. Say you'll make every one in Canva in Rounds 4 and 5 (`shop-look.md`, the image kit).

## Round 2: Their shop (set it up first, or skip if it's ready)

**Before any listing work, find out if their shop exists.** Ask one question:

> Before we write your listing: do you already have a {platform} shop?
> (a) Yes, my shop is open
> (b) I have an account, but no shop yet
> (c) No, I'm starting fresh

**(a) Yes, it's open:**
1. Ask for their shop name and shop link in one message, and save both in their notes.
2. Fill in the "[Your shop name]" spots in their product: show a preview, ask, then save.
3. If the shop is missing basics (icon, banner, About, policies), note them for Round 10.
4. Go to Round 3.

**(b) or (c): walk them through the full setup now,** before the title, description or images. Use the account and shop part of their platform's section in `storefronts.md`:
- **Etsy:** "Open the shop".
- **Stan Store:** Round 2 steps 1 to 3 (profile with a social link or email, then design). Stan connects payments only after the product is published, so that comes in Round 9.
- **Beacons:** steps 1 to 4.
- **Payhip:** steps 1 and 2.
- **Gumroad:** steps 1 and 2.
- **Skool:** `skool.md` Steps 1 to 3.
- **Systeme.io:** sign up and connect payments.
- **Any other platform** (ThriveCart, Podia, Kajabi and so on): `storefronts.md`, "Any other platform". Look up its official steps live, then follow this same shape.

Adding the product itself comes later, in Round 9. Follow the same rules as Round 9: 3 to 5 steps per message, and the identity and money clicks are theirs.

1. **Shop name first: one overarching brand name,** not a product name. Most shops grow to several products for the same buyer, so the name must fit all of them.
   - **Suggest 5 options broad enough for the whole shop:** built from their buyer, their niche or their own name, and short, easy to say and easy to spell.
     - Too narrow: "PaydayPlannerMom", "BillPlannerPrints".
     - Broad enough: "CalmMoneyMama", "TheSimpleBudgetCo", "HappyWalletHome".
   - **One line on why:** "Pick a name that fits everything you'll make for moms like you, not just this planner."
   - **Stay consistent everywhere.** Use the same name, or as close as the platform allows, on:
     - their storefront
     - their social handles (Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok, wherever they'll market)
     - their email
     - later, their own website
   - **Before they fall in love with one,** have them check it's free on their storefront and on the one or two social apps they'll use.
   - **Check the platform's rules.** On Etsy, that's 4 to 20 characters with no spaces or special characters.
   - **Save it in their notes** as their brand name, used everywhere. Then fill in the "[Your shop name]" spots in their product: show a preview, ask, then save.
   - **For a new sign-up, end the naming message with:** "Once you've picked one, I'll give you your link to sign up for {platform}." The name comes first because on many platforms it becomes their web address at sign-up.
2. **The setup steps, a few at a time:** account, shop preferences, getting paid, identity check and security. Wait for "done" or a screenshot after each group.
   - **At the sign-up step, give a clickable "Sign up for {platform} here" link** (Kristin's referral link if she has one for that platform; `storefronts.md`, "Kristin's links", shows exactly how). Never paste the raw web address.
   - Only for new sign-ups. If they already have an account, skip the link.
3. **If setup asks for a first product or listing before the shop can open,** tell them not to worry: "We'll write that listing together next. For now, fill in only what it needs to continue, or save it as a draft, and tell me 'done'." Then come back to it in Round 9.
4. **Their shop's look and words** (`shop-look.md`):
   - **Build what the shop itself shows, in Canva, in their look.** On Etsy that's the shop icon and banner. On other platforms it's the profile photo spot and any header.
   - **Send them the exact color and font settings to copy,** wherever the platform has them.
   - **Shop-level words describe their whole niche, not this one product,** so they still fit when the shop grows. That covers:
     - the shop title or slogan
     - the banner tagline
     - the About section
     - their bio
     - the shop announcement

     Write them from their niche statement (the buyer and the problem they help with), not from the product name. Product words (listing title, description, mockups) come later, in Round 3.
     - Too narrow: "Paycheck budget planner for moms".
     - Broad enough: "Simple budget printables for busy moms", or "Money tools for moms on a budget".
   - **Etsy shop title:**
     - Up to 55 characters. It shows right under the shop name and helps the shop show up on Google (Etsy's help).
     - Suggest 3 niche-level options that carry a broad niche keyword ("budget printables", not "paycheck budget planner"). They pick one.
     - Where: Shop Manager → the **pencil icon** next to the shop name under **Sales Channels** → **Add a shop title**.
5. **Settings to decide now, one line each:**
   - Etsy's Offsite Ads choice (they can opt out while under $10,000 a year in sales; `storefronts.md`).
   - Their currency, which should match their bank's.

**Shop setup is done only when every one of these is true.** Check them off, and save progress in their notes as you go:
- [ ] The shop is open, and its name and link are in their notes
- [ ] The shop name is filled into their product (previewed, approved, saved)
- [ ] The shop icon and banner (Etsy) or profile photo and header are built in Canva and uploaded
- [ ] Colors and fonts are set, where the platform has them
- [ ] The shop title or slogan is set, and it's about their niche, not one product
- [ ] Offsite Ads are decided (Etsy), and the currency matches their bank

If they want to skip the icon or banner for now, that's their choice. Note it for Finishing touches, and move on.
Only then mark Shop setup ✓, and say in one line: "Your shop is ready. Now let's write the listing that sells your planner."

## Round 3: Package it (the title and description)

Write everything from their promise sentence and their buyer's words. Show it in the chat, in short labeled sections.

**Title.** Kristin's formula: *what it is, who it's for, what it helps with.*
- Their main keyword first, in natural everyday words (Day 5: about 60 to 80 characters).
- **Etsy:** up to 140 characters. Etsy suggests fewer than 15 words and no subjective words like "perfect" or "best".
- **Everywhere else:** a clear product name, plus a headline that says the result.
- Give 3 options and recommend one. Kristin's check: would someone instantly understand it? Does it feel specific and valuable? Would it stand out the first time someone sees it?

**Description.** Kristin's rule: *sell the result, not just the product.* Use her structure, which follows the gap from where they are to where they want to be (`why-people-buy.md`, section 9):
1. **Call out the problem** in the buyer's own words, so they feel seen.
2. **What staying stuck costs them,** in one gentle, honest line. That's real urgency, never pressure (section 10).
3. **Introduce the product** as the bridge.
4. **Explain the result:** what changes, in things they can see.
5. **List what's included:** the parts, benefit first.

Then add:
- **This is for you if... / This isn't for you if...** (section 11).
- **What you can expect:** the visible result in plain words. Never reviews, testimonials or invented results (`rules.md`, rule 3).
- **What they get and how:** the format, the number of pages, and "instant digital download, nothing will be shipped".
- **What they need:** a printer, a free Canva account, a tablet app...
- **Usage:** personal use only, unless they've decided otherwise.
- **Etsy only: an honest AI note in the description,** if AI helped make it. Etsy requires it (see `storefronts.md`). **Write it from how they really made it,** in their voice: what was theirs (the idea, their choices, any words they wrote, their own tips) and what AI tools helped with (drafting the words, organizing, the design). Their notes say most of it; if they don't say who wrote the words, ask in one line. Examples:
  - They wrote the words: "My idea and my words, organized and designed with help from AI tools."
  - You drafted the words: "My idea and my choices, written and designed with help from AI tools."
  - Their own steps (a how-to): "Steps written by me, design made with help from AI tools."

  **Never write that AI, or you, created or made their product.** They made it, with your help (`rules.md`, rule 5). Show them the note and ask "Does this match how you made it?" before it goes in the listing (Kristin, Oct 25: a student's idea and wording were all hers, and her guide said it had created the product).
  Stan, Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad, Systeme.io and Skool don't ask for one, so leave it out there unless the student wants it.
- **A kind no-refund line** (Round 7).

**Platform extras:**
- **Etsy:** 13 tags from their keyword set (3 or more words each where possible, up to 20 characters each), plus every attribute that applies. The main keyword goes in the first line of the description too.
- **Stan, Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad (storefront product pages):** use the order in `sales-pages.md`, as **plain text only.**
  - The platform puts its own buy button on the page. That's the only button.
  - Never write button markers like "[Get my budget planner]" or links inside the description. Stan's description can't hold clickable buttons or links (Kristin, October 2026).
  - Start with a call-out line naming the buyer, a short result headline and a subheadline carrying the main keyword.
  - Use short paragraphs and simple lists typed as text, so it reads well in a plain box.
  - Label every piece with the box it goes in. **Stan** (box names from Stan's help, checked October 4, 2026):
    - **Thumbnail tab:** title, subtitle, button text ("Get my budget planner")
    - **Description:** the call-out line and the result headline
    - **Description Body:** everything else, top to bottom
    - **Bottom Button Title:** the closing line that repeats the result ("Every bill, planned.")
    - **Call-to-Action Button text:** the same words as the thumbnail button
    - Stan's help doesn't say what each box shows. If their screen looks different, ask what they see.
  - **Beacons, Payhip, Gumroad:** one product name, one description box and the platform's own button. Ask what boxes they see, and label the copy to match.
- **Systeme.io (a page you build):** the full `sales-pages.md` layout, with the same benefit button after every section (for example "Get my budget planner"). You build the buttons with the connector.
- **No fake urgency or scarcity, ever.** A deadline only if it's true.

**Before you show it, check it:**
- **Words** (`why-people-buy.md`, section 14): a 4th or 5th grade reading level, nothing vague, none of the words to cut, in their voice.
- **Keywords** (section 15): the main keyword is in the title, the first lines and (on a page) the subheadline, and it reads naturally.

> Here are your title and description{, and tags}. Want to change anything, or shall I make your mockups?

If they feel shy or "salesy" about it, one line from `why-people-buy.md` section 12 helps: if it truly helps, sharing it is helping.
Save the approved title, description and tags in their notes.

## Round 4: Package it (mockups, built in Canva)

Kristin's rule: *customers can't "see" a digital product, so mockups make it feel real.* A mockup shows their product in real life: printed on a desk, on a tablet, on a phone, on a clipboard on the fridge.
Kristin's list of what mockups should show: **what's included, how it works, what they can edit, and what to expect.** Keep them simple, clean, easy to read, and not overdesigned.

**Build them in Canva with the Canva connector,** in the product's look (style sheet), as one design with one image per page:
1. **Hero mockup:** the product in a real-life scene, plus its name and the promise in a few words. This is the thumbnail buyers see first, so it must read clearly at a tiny size. Build this one first and show it as a sneak peek.
2. **Lifestyle mockup:** the product being used by the kind of person who buys it (Day 5's "lifestyle scene").
3. **What's included:** all the parts at a glance (for example, the pages fanned out).
4. **How it works:** 3 simple steps, or (templates) what they can edit.
5. **What you can expect:** the visible result. Never a review or a made-up result.

**Always use their real pages.** Never show pages that don't exist. Build every listing photo in their Canva, with their real pages placed by you, so they can edit any of it and download it from Canva.
Tested with Kristin on claude.ai in October 2026. It worked once Claude's network access was on.

**How their pages get into their Canva, easiest first:**
1. **You do it all.**
   - Export the pages the photos need from their product design (Canva connector).
   - Get the files, then upload each one into their Canva with the Canva upload tool. That's create-upload-url, then the file sent to that address.
   - Place them in your layouts.
2. **They drop the pages in the chat.** Use this if you can't get the export files yourself:
   > Send me pictures of your pages. In Canva: Share → Download → PNG → Select pages {list} → Download. Open the zip and drag the images in here.

   Then you upload and place them, the same way.
3. **If uploading to Canva is blocked,** the cause is Claude's network setting. Explain it once, and let them choose:
   > To put your real pages into Canva for you, Claude needs permission to send files there. It's a one-time switch:
   > - **Settings** → **Capabilities** → **Allow network egress**.
   > - On a Team plan, the owner turns it on in **Admin settings** → **Capabilities**.
   > - If it lets you list websites, add only **canva.com**.
   >
   > Claude warns that network access comes with security risks, so only turn it on if you're comfortable. Afterwards, start a new chat in your project and type **Keep going**. (If you can't save their notes yourself, hand them their notes first, `SKILL.md` section 2.)
4. **If they'd rather not,** use one of these:
   - **They upload, you place:**
     1. They download the pages as PNG.
     2. In the listing-images design: **Uploads** → **Upload files**, all at once.
     3. They click each page once, so it lands on the page. Anywhere is fine.
     4. They say "done".
     5. You find those images, then move, size and tilt them into place, and reuse them in every photo.
   - **Canva's Mockups tool,** for one realistic scene, with no uploading: Canva homepage → **Apps** → **Mockups** → search "printed paper", "clipboard" or "tablet" → **Select** → **Designs** → their product design → **Select**.

**Then build the whole set in that one Canva design:**
- Every photo at the right size: on Etsy, 2400 x 1800 landscape or 2000 x 2000 square.
- Their real pages reused across all the photos.
- **Leftover pages: you delete them, never them.** Before you say the set is saved, read the design back. If a page isn't one of their images (a spare cover from setup, a blank page), ask once:
  > There's a spare page left over from how I set up your design (page {n}). Want me to delete it, so only your images are in there?

  On their yes, delete it yourself with the Canva connector's merge-designs tool (delete_pages), then read the design back to check. Never tell them to delete it, skip it or ignore it.
- Show a preview, ask, save. **Every message that says something was saved includes the Canva link to that design,** starting with the first image. Never just the design's name.

They change anything they like in Canva, then **Share** → **Download** → **JPG**. Downloading from Canva works in the browser, the desktop app and on a phone.

Guide them 3 to 5 steps per message, and check the result yourself afterwards (read the design back).

**Sizes: make the whole image kit for their storefront** (`shop-look.md`), not only listing photos.
**Make only the images their storefront has a spot for.** Never build a set the platform can't show. Pictures for social posts come later, in Market.
- **Etsy:** listing photos 2400 x 1800 landscape (or 2000 x 2000 square) with the product centered, a 4:1 shop banner and a 500 x 500 icon. JPGs under about 1 MB.
- **Stan Store: 2 images only.** Stan's help lists just two image spots for a digital product, and no gallery (checked October 4, 2026):
  - the **400 x 400 thumbnail** on the store card. This is what buyers see first on their store page, so it must read at a tiny size: the cover plus a few big words.
  - the **1920 x 1080 checkout banner,** at the top of the checkout page: the hero mockup, which can show a few real pages.
- **Systeme.io:** a sales page hero image.
- **Gumroad:** a 1280 x 720 cover and a thumbnail.
- **Skool:** course covers and About page graphics.

Build the main set once, then resize it for each spot. Resizing needs Canva Pro. On Canva Free, create each size as its own design in the same look.
If they have a Canva Brand Kit, ask once whether to use it.

**Optional:** their shop name, small and subtle, as a watermark.

> Here's your first mockup, the hero image buyers see first: {Canva link}.
> It's image 1 of {their kit's count: about 8 to 10 on Etsy, 2 on Stan}. Once you like this look, I'll make the rest: {only the images their storefront has a spot for. On Etsy, e.g. a lifestyle scene, what's included, how it works, what you can expect, then close-ups of your real pages. On Stan, just the 400 x 400 store card}.
> Love it? Say "make the rest", or tell me what to change.

## Round 5: Package it (preview images)

Preview images are the honest, close-up look inside. Kristin's list: *show real product pages, highlight the inside content, give a closer look.*

**Only where their storefront has room for more images:** Etsy, Payhip, Gumroad, Beacons or a Systeme.io page. **On Stan, this round becomes "Checkout page".** Stan has no image gallery, only the thumbnail and the checkout banner, so the banner can show a few real pages. Then offer the designed checkout page choice (all text, all images, or both) from `shop-look.md`, "Stan Store: a designed checkout page". Show it as **Checkout page** on their roadmap, not Preview images. If they pick all text, it's done in one line.
Add these to the same Canva design, after the mockups:
1. **2 or 3 real pages,** shown flat and big enough to read.
2. **The most useful page,** up close, with one short label ("Your paycheck plan page").
3. **Marketplaces like Etsy:**
   - "Digital download, nothing will be shipped" (this saves confused buyers and bad reviews)
   - "Instructions included" (or "How to download")

On Etsy, mockups and preview images together make about 8 to 10 images.
- **Etsy's image rules** (updated October 22, 2026, `storefronts.md`): the real pages in point 1 are the preview Etsy requires for a digital download, so never skip them. No image may make the product look better or different than the file.
- Etsy allows up to 20 photos and 2 short videos. 8 to 10 strong images is plenty, so never pad with weak ones.
- Don't claim more photos (or anything else) makes Etsy show a listing to more people, unless Etsy's own help says so and you link it.
- Number the image plan simply, 1 to 10, one image per line.
They save them from Canva as JPG or PNG (Share → Download). Etsy doesn't show transparent PNGs correctly, so use JPG or a solid background there. Name each image file with the main keyword before uploading (`budget-planner-printable-monthly-page.jpg`), and use the same words as alt text wherever the platform asks for it.
If Canva won't connect, describe each image (what it shows, the words on it) and help them build it from a Canva template, or make the images yourself as files.

## Round 6: Price it right

They set a launch price and a regular price in Research. Now confirm it with them, using Kristin's pricing beats:
- **Pricing is strategy, not just a number.** It shapes how valuable the product looks.
- **Underpricing hurts:** it lowers the value people see, attracts the wrong buyers, and makes growth harder.
- **Sell the outcome, not the file.** Price for what it does for them, not for the hours it took.
- **Pricing psychology:** $9 feels easier than $10. Simple pricing converts.
- **Keep it simple:** most first products sit around $7 to $47. They can adjust over time.
- **Bundles raise the value:** 2 or 3 related products together (for example, the planner plus a grocery list pack) can be priced higher with confidence. Only bundle products that already exist.

**Check the price still matches what's inside** (Kristin's test, Oct 17). Compare the pages in their real design with what Research priced. If the product is smaller, say so honestly, once, and then:
- **If a missing piece is the change Research said makes theirs stand out** (a "Tweak" verdict), recommend building it **before launch** and keeping the price. Say how long it takes, and offer to make it now. Afterwards, update everything that describes what's inside: the description, the preview images and their notes.
- **If they'd rather launch as it is,** recommend the price that fits what it is now, from the matching part of the Research range, and raise it once the piece is added.
- **Never justify a price with the sales page** ("your page is strong"). Price follows what's inside, what it does for them and what buyers pay for similar products.

Then show, as a rough estimate from the platform's current official fees.
**Work every number out with code if you can, and check each row before you show it.** A wrong "you keep" number damages trust. Give one line of the math for the first row, for example "$7 − $0.46 − $0.46 − $0.20".

> At {price}, you'd keep about {amount} from each sale on {platform}, after {fees}.
> Launch at {launch price}, then move to {regular price} after your first 10 sales. Still feel right?

The launch price is a real plan, so they can mention it honestly, for example "Launch price for the first 10 sales". In your words to them: "Just don't add a countdown or a deadline that isn't real."

**Then one quick question: is there a second product?**
**If they made an interactive version in Execute** (a fillable or printable version, in their notes), that's their second product: skip the question and go straight to "Yes, and it's made" below.
Ask only if their notes don't already say:

> One more: do you have another product that helps the same buyer, like {a natural next product, `why-people-buy.md` section 13}? If you do, we can offer it as an add-on when people buy. If not, no problem, we'll note the idea for later.

- **Yes, and it's made:** recommend **one** add-on that fits their platform (`upsells-and-funnels.md`, part 1). Usually that's a bundle on Etsy and an order bump everywhere else, priced below the main product. Write its few lines now, in the same voice. Save it in their notes.
  - **One listing at a time.** The main product goes live first (Round 9). On Etsy, the second product's own listing and the bundle follow right after, reusing the same steps. Say so in one line, so they never juggle three listings at once.
- **Not yet:** note the idea in their Decisions and move on. Finishing and selling the first product comes first.

## Round 7: Protect your product

Kristin's three, plus one Etsy check. Make every change for them, showing it first and getting their OK before saving.
1. **A copyright page (or section) inside the product.** It sets clear ownership. Put it on the closing page, or as a short final page:
   - **Printables and ebooks:** *"© {year} {shop name}. For personal use only. Please don't share, copy or resell this file. Thank you for supporting a small business!"*
   - **Template packs:** *"Edit these for your own business. Please don't share or resell the templates themselves."*
   - **No closing page** (a short printable, like a cover and one planner page): put the full line in the cover's footer, and a short version in the footer of every page they'll print, *"© {year} {shop name} · Personal use only"*, because the printed pages are the ones that get passed around (Kristin's test, Oct 17).
2. **Watermarks (optional), so ask in one line, and let them choose:** "Want your shop name as a small watermark on your listing photos? It's optional." Never claim what "most shops" do. A watermark is subtle branding on listing images only. Never inside the paid product, and kept minimal.
3. **A no-refund policy:** digital products are final.
   - Set it where the platform allows, add it to the shop policies, and turn on a terms checkbox if the platform has one.
   - Keep the wording human: *"Because this is an instant digital download, all sales are final. If anything goes wrong opening your file, message me and I'll make it right."*
   - **Stan:** the line goes in the checkout copy (Round 3). Stan's **Terms & Conditions** (store-wide, paid plans, shown above the checkout button) are turned on in Round 9, right after connecting payments, because it's the same tab (`storefronts.md`, Stan). Tell them that in one line, so Protect can be marked done now.
4. **Every shop mention inside the product points to where they really sell.** Read every page: an old link or "Find me on…" left from another platform is easy to miss.
   - Use the exact shop link from their notes. Never guess one from their shop name. If it's not in their notes, ask them to copy it from their browser.
   - **Selling on Etsy?** The "What's next" and About me pages must point only to their Etsy shop, never to another store or website. Etsy doesn't allow sending buyers elsewhere to buy. Update the design if needed.

## Round 8: Deliver your product

**They choose how buyers get it,** from Kristin's delivery options. Explain each option in one line, recommend the one that fits their product and platform, and build what's needed. The choice is theirs.

**The one rule: the buyer gets exactly what the student chose, and what the listing promised.**
- If the listing says "printable PDF", a PDF arrives.
- If they chose a Canva view link, the listing and the thank-you message say so in plain words: "You'll get a link to view your planner in Canva."
- Write the "What they get and how" line in Round 3 from this choice, and update it if the choice changes.

| Option | What the buyer gets | A good fit for | Worth knowing (one line each) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The file itself (PDF, images)** | The file, downloaded from the platform | Printables, ebooks, planners, graphics, within the platform's limits (Etsy: 5 files, 20 MB each) | Printables: PDF Print. Screens and clickable links: PDF Standard (Execute's save table). |
| **Canva public view link** | A view-only link that opens the design in Canva, with no login, and always shows the latest version | Products they'll keep updating, freebies, products meant to be viewed on screen | Anyone with the link can see it. Before choosing it, open it signed out and see what a buyer can actually do (view, print, download), then say exactly that in the listing. |
| **Canva template link** | Their own editable copy; the original stays protected | Canva template packs, editable planners and worksheets | Needs Canva Pro. Deliver it inside an access PDF, or as the redirect link on Stan or Beacons. |
| **Thank-you PDF (and bundles)** | A one-page PDF with one clear button per item | Bundles, several files, anything delivered by links | The buyer has one simple place to go. |
| **PDF + Google Drive** | A Drive link to the file or a folder, shared as "Anyone with the link: **Viewer**" | Big files, bundles over the platform's limits, Systeme.io funnels | Works together with the thank-you PDF: the PDF is the delivery page, Drive is the storage. |

**How to recommend:**
- **A printable to print at home:** the file itself.
- **A template pack:** a template link.
- **A bundle:** a thank-you PDF.
- **Something they'll keep updating:** a view link can work well.

**Show the options, one line each, with your pick first.** Only the ones that work on their platform (`delivery.md`, section 1). For example, a printable on Stan:

> Here's how moms could get your planner. My pick is (a).
> - **(a) The PDF file** (my pick): they download it right after paying, and it comes by email too. It matches what your listing promises.
> - **(b) A Canva view link:** it opens your planner in Canva and always shows your latest version. Anyone with the link can see it.
> - **(c) A Canva template link:** each buyer gets their own editable copy. It needs Canva Pro, and it fits templates more than printables.
> - **(d) A thank-you PDF:** one page with a button for each item. Best for bundles.
> - **(e) PDF + Google Drive:** for files too big to upload.
>
> Which one would you like?

Never wave the others off in one line ("they don't fit your planner"). Then let them choose. If they pick a different option, go with it, and update the listing so it says what arrives.

**Then follow `delivery.md`** to set up the option they chose on their platform, step by step.

**Build the thank-you PDF (or access PDF) for them in Canva,** in the product's look:
- the product name and a warm thank you
- one big, clear button per item, labeled with what it opens ("Open your budget planner", "Open your bill tracker pack")
- 3 quick steps for using it
- where to get help
- the copyright line

Check every link in it. Then they save it as PDF Standard (links keep working).
**Whenever they paste a link for delivery, check it opens the right product before you use it.**
- Expand Canva shortlinks with the Canva connector.
- Open the link signed out if you can.

In Kristin's test, the first link pasted for the add-on opened the main product instead.

**Kristin's warning, every time:** never share a Canva link set to "Anyone with the link can **edit**". It hands everyone the original design. Template links and view links only.

**File names:** rename files to the product name before uploading, for example `Budget-Planner-Printable.pdf`. On Etsy, names can't be changed after upload: up to 70 characters, letters, numbers, periods, underscores or hyphens.

## Round 9: List it

Their shop was set up in Round 2, or already existed. Now add the product. Walk them through the listing or product part of their platform's section in `storefronts.md`:
- **Etsy:** "Add the listing".
- **Stan Store:** Round 9 steps 1 to 8 (the product, then payments, then Terms & Conditions).
- **Beacons:** steps 5 and 6.
- **Payhip:** steps 3 to 6.
- **Gumroad:** steps 3 to 9.
- **Any other platform:** its official steps, looked up live (`storefronts.md`, "Any other platform").

Then:
- **Hand them a short list of which image goes where** (`shop-look.md`): the hero mockup first, then the rest in order.
- **On Systeme.io,** you build the pages in their look, and they swap in the mockups.
- **Skool:** follow `skool.md`, the full build (brand, pricing, payouts, classroom, Start Here post, About page and more).
- **Systeme.io:** if they connect Systeme.io's connector, build the product, funnel and emails for them, showing each piece first (see `storefronts.md`). If its page templates ask for testimonials or credentials they don't have, build the pages without templates.
- **The email buyers get, before the product is published** (`buyer-email.md`):
  - Write it from their notes, in their voice: the product version (Stan, Beacons, Gumroad, Systeme.io) or the shop version (Etsy, Payhip).
  - Show it, get their OK, then hand it over box by box and walk them through their platform's setup.
  - **Stan:** it's step 5, in the product's **Options** tab, before **Publish**.
  - Save "Email buyers get: set" in their notes.
- **Their add-on (if they chose one in Round 6):** set it up on their platform with `upsells-and-funnels.md`, part 1. The box is never ticked for the buyer, the price is clear, and the add-on reaches only the people who bought it.
- **Every platform:**
  - **3 to 5 steps per message,** then wait for "done" or a screenshot.
  - When a step needs their title, description, tags, images, price or file, hand it over ready to paste or upload, **in full, in that message.** Never "copy it from your notes". Their notes are for remembering, not for copying from (Kristin's test, Oct 17).
  - **The product goes in the way they chose in Round 8** (`delivery.md`): the file uploaded, or the link pasted where the platform takes links.
  - **The clicks that involve their identity and money are theirs:** creating accounts, ID checks, bank details, card details, tax forms.
    - Never ask them to type passwords, card numbers, bank numbers or ID photos into the chat.
    - If they're stuck on a screen, a screenshot with private details covered is enough.
  - **Screens change.** If what they see doesn't match, ask what they see and help from there.

## Round 10: Finishing touches

Write these, ready to paste, in one message:
- **About section:** 3 or 4 warm sentences: a real person, what the shop specializes in (their whole niche, not one product), what buyers can expect. From their notes and story only, with no invented credentials.
- **FAQ:**
  - How do I receive my product? The answer always says it's a digital download, names the file type (and paper size for printables), and says "Nothing will be shipped", so no one expects a package (Kristin's test, Oct 17).
  - Do I need any special software or apps?
  - Can I print this at home? (printables)
  - Can I share it?
  - What if I have trouble opening it?
  - Do you offer refunds?
- **Shop policies:** refund (Round 7), delivery, usage rights, and how to get help.
- **The email buyers get** was set up in Round 9 (`buyer-email.md`). If it was skipped, do it now. On Etsy, a thank-you offer can also hint at what's next (`upsells-and-funnels.md`).
- **Etsy only:** a shop announcement, niche-level (Shop Manager → the pencil icon next to the shop name → **Announcement**). Their shop title was set in Round 2. If it wasn't, do it now.
- **Stan only (no FAQ or About box):** the bio was set in Round 2. Give your pick, as always: put the FAQ at the end of Description Body **in place of** the must-know lines, because it covers the same facts in a friendlier way. Never use both, or buyers read the same lines twice. The About lines are saved for their Instagram bio and posts in Market (Kristin's test, Oct 17).

Two habits to share in one line each:
- **Reviews:** ask for an honest review, but **never offer anything in return for one**, and never write reviews for them.
- **Fast replies:** answer buyer messages within a day. On Etsy it counts toward Star Seller, and everywhere it builds trust.

## Round 11: Check it like a buyer

> Last step before we call it open. Let's look at it the way a buyer will:
> 1. Open your listing on your phone. Does the first image make sense as a tiny thumbnail? Read the title and the first lines of the description. On a sales page: is the headline 3 lines or fewer, and can you see the button without scrolling?
> 2. Check the delivery works: {the test that fits their platform}. **What arrives must match what you chose and what your listing promised:** the PDF downloads, the view link opens your design, the template link makes you your own copy.
> 3. Click every link in your thank-you or access PDF {in a private browser window} to see what a buyer sees.
> 4. Open the email a buyer gets, on your phone: the download works, and your name, shop and email are right (`buyer-email.md`, section 4).

The test that fits:
- **Gumroad:** use its test purchase feature. Never buy your own product with a real card there.
- **Etsy:** use **Preview** (Etsy won't let sellers buy from their own shop).
- **Stan:** Stan's own test, before they share their store: set the price to $0, publish, "buy" it with their own email, check the thank-you page and the email, then **set the price back and publish again** (`buyer-email.md`, section 4).
- **Beacons, Payhip, Systeme.io:** preview the product and checkout pages, and open the delivered file or link yourself. If their plan has discount codes, a 100%-off code is an easy way to run a real test checkout.

If they have an add-on, check it too: the box starts unticked, the total is right, and the add-on arrives only when it's chosen.
Fix anything they find.
