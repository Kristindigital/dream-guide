## Country guide (snapshot, October 2026)

| Where they live | Usually works best |
|---|---|
| US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, most of Europe, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong | Everything works. Choose by goals (Step 2). |
| EU and UK, and they don't want to deal with digital VAT | Etsy, Gumroad, Payhip or Skool. These handle the VAT for them. On Stan, Beacons or Systeme.io, VAT is their job. |
| Mexico | Etsy, Stan (Stripe), Gumroad, Payhip. |
| India | Etsy (with Payoneer) or Gumroad (pays to Indian bank accounts, and Indian buyers can pay by UPI). Stan works through PayPal only. Skool also pays out in India, for a community or course. |
| Pakistan | Etsy (with Payoneer) or Gumroad (pays to Pakistani bank accounts). Stripe isn't available, and PayPal may not be: check before choosing Stan, Beacons or Payhip. |
| Philippines, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam | Etsy or Gumroad. Stan works through PayPal (Stripe isn't available on Stan there). |
| South Africa | Etsy or Gumroad (pays in rand). Selar is a local option for selling to South African buyers. |
| Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya | Etsy is not available. **Gumroad** (pays to local bank accounts), or **Selar** for selling to local buyers in local currency. Stan, Beacons and Payhip only if their PayPal can receive money. |
| Brazil and the rest of Latin America | Etsy (Brazil, Argentina, Chile and Peru need Payoneer), Gumroad, or **Hotmart**, the big local platform. |
| Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and anywhere not listed | Gumroad first (check their country is on Gumroad's list), then PayPal-based options if PayPal can receive there. |

Taxes: platforms handle different parts of sales tax and VAT. Explain what the platform does in one line, then suggest they check their own country's rules or ask a local accountant. Never give tax or legal advice as fact.

## Step 2: Match the platform to their goals

These come from Kristin's storefront guide ("built for" and "why this is for you").

| If they... | Recommend | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Want buyers to find them through search, without relying on social media | **Etsy** (if their country is supported). Otherwise **Gumroad** or **Payhip**. | Etsy has millions of buyers already searching. Fees per sale instead of a monthly bill. |
| Want to sell from their social media (link in bio), spend nothing to start, and test ideas | **Beacons** (free plan) or **Payhip** (free plan) | Free to start; a small fee per sale (Beacons 9%, Payhip 5%). |
| Have or are building a social audience, want the simplest setup, and are happy to pay monthly | **Stan Store** | Clean, fast, no Stan fee per sale. $29 a month after a 14-day trial. |
| Want email marketing, funnels and everything in one place, and don't mind learning a little tech | **Systeme.io** | Free plan with email, funnels and digital delivery. No transaction fees. Your guide can build most of it with them through the Systeme.io connector. |
| Want a community, a course or membership, or a free community that also sells their product inside | **Skool** (full guide: `skool.md`) | Community, classroom and calls in one place. A free group can sell their product as a one-time "Buy now" course. |
| Want the fastest, no-tech start, or live where other platforms can't pay them | **Gumroad** | No monthly fee, pays out almost everywhere, handles worldwide sales tax. |
| Want to build a full branded store for many products, long term | **Shopify** (later, not usually the first store) | Powerful and scalable, but more setup and a monthly fee. |

Rules of thumb:
- **Recommend one platform now**, plus one backup. Kristin's rule: *your first storefront doesn't have to be forever.*
- **Search + social later is a great path.** Start on Etsy for search traffic and add a link-in-bio store when they start posting (Market).
- **Product type matters.** Template packs need somewhere to hold a Canva link (all of these work, with their access PDF). Courses and memberships fit Skool, Systeme.io, Stan, Beacons Creator Plus, Payhip or Gumroad better than Etsy. Build the course itself with `course-building.md`, whichever platform they pick.
- **Some platforms let you build for them.** If Systeme.io's connector is connected, you can build much of their store directly (see Systeme.io). For the others, you write everything and they click.
- **Etsy needs their own designs.** PLR content sold as-is breaks Etsy's rules (digital items must be made or designed by the seller).

## Etsy

**Best for:** search traffic without social media. **Tech:** beginner friendly.
**Costs (official, Oct 2026):**
- A one-time shop set-up fee. Etsy shows the amount during setup; it varies by country.
- $0.20 per listing, which lasts 4 months and auto-renews. A listing with quantity renews for another $0.20 after each sale.
- 6.5% transaction fee on each sale.
- A payment processing fee that depends on their bank's country (shown in Etsy's Payments Policy).
- In some countries, a Regulatory Operating fee.
- Offsite Ads: 15% on sales Etsy's outside ads bring in, only on those sales. They can opt out while under $10,000 a year in sales. Above that it becomes mandatory at 12%.
- 2.5% currency conversion if they price in a different currency from their bank. Tell them to price in their bank's currency.
- Official: https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees

**Rules that matter for digital sellers:**
- Digital items must be made or designed by the seller.
- **If AI helped create it, say so.** Etsy requires seller-prompted AI creations to disclose the use of AI. In the "How it's made" section choose the option that fits (designed by them), and add one honest line to the description, like "Designed by me, with help from AI tools."
- **No sending buyers elsewhere to buy** (no links or QR codes to another shop). The "What's next" page inside a product sold on Etsy should point to their Etsy shop, not to Stan or a website.
- No variations on digital listings. One file set per listing.

**Before they start, they need:** a computer (Etsy's shop setup must be done in a desktop browser), a government photo ID, a bank account in their own name, a debit or credit card for Etsy's fees, and their phone for two-step login.

**Open the shop: Etsy's current steps.** Re-checked on Etsy's own help page on October 3, 2026: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015672808

Send these 3 to 5 at a time, then wait for "done" or a screenshot. Everything happens on a computer, in a web browser.
1. **Create their Etsy account** if they don't have one: on Etsy.com, **Sign in** → **Register** (or continue with Google, Facebook or Apple). A profile photo and short bio are optional, but they build trust.
2. Go to **Etsy.com/sell** → **Get started**, and answer the few questions about their selling experience.
3. **Shop preferences:**
   - **Shop language:** it can't be changed later.
   - **Shop country.**
   - **Shop currency:** match their bank's currency to avoid conversion fees.
4. **Shop name:** type the name they chose.
   - The rules: 4 to 20 characters, no spaces or special characters, not already taken, and not someone else's trademark.
   - If it's taken, Etsy suggests names that are free. Help them pick, then update the name in their product.
   - The name is held while they finish, and can be changed later (**Your shop** → **Shop Name**).
5. **Get paid:**
   - **Where is your bank located:** choose their bank's country.
   - **For tax purposes, what type of seller are you:** most beginners choose **Individual or sole proprietorship**.
   - Add their bank details. They type these into Etsy, never into this chat.
6. **Billing:** add a debit or credit card for Etsy's fees.
7. **Set-up fee:** a one-time, non-refundable fee. Check the box in **Total due now** to authorize it. Etsy shows the amount there.
8. **Verify identity (Persona):**
   - Choose **Express review** (with biometric info) or **Manual review** (without).
   - **Begin verifying** → the country that issued their ID → choose the ID.
   - The ID must not be expired, and **its name must match the name on the bank account they added.**
   - Upload the ID photo and take a selfie. They can switch to their phone with **Use another device**.
   - They can keep setting up while it's reviewed. The status shows later in **Shop Manager** → **Legal & tax information**.
9. **Two-factor login:** an authenticator app is recommended, or a text or call. If the phone number won't go in, use an authenticator app.
10. Select **Open your shop**.

**Then the storefront, built with them right away** (Etsy's "set up your storefront"):
- **Shop icon and banner:** build both in Canva, in their look (`shop-look.md`), then walk them through uploading (Etsy's help, checked October 3, 2026: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015663247).
  - **Banner:** Shop Manager → the **pencil icon** next to their shop name under **Sales Channels** → **Banner** → choose **Big banner** → upload → **Done**.
  - **Shop icon (Etsy calls it the logo):** Shop Manager → **Settings** → **Your shop** → under **Logo**, **Add** → **Add file** → crop → **Save**.
  - Tell them in one line that Etsy says a shop without a logo may show up less in search.
  - **Save both from Canva as JPG.** Etsy warns that images over 1 MB may not finish uploading, and transparent PNGs show black.
- **About and policies:** can wait for Round 10.

**Tell them once:** buyers can't see the shop until the first listing is added. That's Round 9, after we write and design it. If setup insists on a listing before it continues, save a draft with the bare minimum, and fill it in properly in Round 9.

**Add the listing.** In Shop Manager → **Listings** → **Add a listing**. The form has 7 sections:
1. **Photo & video:** up to 20 photos and 2 short videos (5 to 15 seconds).
   - **The first photo is the search thumbnail.** Etsy wants it landscape or square, and leans toward landscape. Images should be at least 2000 px.
   - Keep the product in the center with space around it, because Etsy crops thumbnails to square, portrait and landscape. Use the thumbnail adjustment tool in the listing to check.
   - Use the same shape for every photo.
   - Use JPGs under about 1 MB.
2. **Category:** pick the closest category, then **Digital files** for "What type of item is it?".
3. **Item details:** title (up to 140 characters; Etsy suggests under 15 words, keyword first, no words like "perfect"), digital files (up to 5 files, 20MB each), description.
4. **Item options:** attributes (fill in every one that applies; beginners don't know which do, so ask for a screenshot of the attribute fields and tell them exactly what to pick) and up to 13 tags (their buyer phrases from Research, 3+ words each where possible).
5. **Pricing & shipping:** price and quantity. For digital items, Kristin's tip is a high quantity (like 999) so the listing never sells out.
6. **How it's made:** who made it and how (see the AI rule above).
7. **Settings:** shop section, and renewal set to **Automatic**.
Then **Preview**, check everything, and **Publish**. It can take up to 48 hours to show in search.

**Important details:**
- **File names can't be changed after upload.** Up to 70 characters, letters, numbers, periods, underscores or hyphens. Rename the file before uploading (for example `Budget-Planner-Printable.pdf`).
- The listing's web address is set by the title the first time it's published.
- Over 20MB, or more than 5 files? Use a thank-you PDF with a Google Drive link (see delivery in `step-apply.md`).
- Etsy won't let sellers buy from their own shop. Use **Preview** to check the listing.
- Digital purchases are automatically non-returnable on Etsy, but they can still choose to help an unhappy buyer.

**Then complete the shop:** shop icon/logo, banner, About section, shop announcement, FAQ, policies, and a message to buyers. A complete shop builds trust.
**Add-ons on Etsy:** there's no add-on at checkout. Use a bundle listing, or a thank-you offer (`upsells-and-funnels.md`).
SEO, ads and growth come later, in Market.

**Best for:** selling from social media with the simplest setup. **Tech:** beginner friendly.
**Costs (Oct 2026):** Creator $29/month or Creator Pro $99/month (yearly: $300 or $948). 14-day free trial. **Stan takes no cut of sales**; Stripe or PayPal charge their normal processing fee. Creator Pro adds discount codes, order bumps, payment plans, and email flows and broadcasts. Official pricing: https://stan.store
**Get paid:** Stripe (a Stan-managed account, only in the countries listed in Step 1) or PayPal. They must be 18 or older. Choose the right country: it can't be changed later in settings.
Kristin's link: https://join.stan.store/KristinHorsburgh (disclose it).

**Stan's own steps,** checked on help.stan.store on October 4, 2026. Send them 3 to 5 at a time.

**Important order on Stan:** Stan only lets them connect Stripe or PayPal **after at least one product is published,** and after a social link or their email is on their profile. So the shop part (Round 2) is the profile and design, and payments come straight after the product goes live (Round 9). Tell them this in one line, so it doesn't feel like a missed step.

**Round 2, the shop:**
1. **Sign up with Kristin's link** (disclose it) and start the 14-day trial.
2. **Profile:** **My Store** → the pencil next to their name.
   - Add their photo, their shop or brand name, and a short niche-level bio (who they help, not one product; see `shop-look.md`).
   - **Add at least one social link, or their email.** Stripe and PayPal need it to verify them.
   - Save.
3. **Edit Design:** a theme, colors and fonts from their style sheet (`shop-look.md`).

**Round 9, the product, then payments:**
1. **My Store** → **Add Product** → **Digital Product**.
2. **Thumbnail tab:**
   - the style: **Callout** (Kristin's pick, Oct 17). It shows their image, title, subtitle and a big button, so all of Round 3's thumbnail words show up. Stan also offers Button and Preview; name Callout as the pick, never "whichever shows your image biggest".
   - the thumbnail image (**400 x 400**)
   - the title, subtitle and button text (Round 3's words)
3. **Checkout Page tab:**
   - **Banner image (the image spot at the top):** the 1920 x 1080 banner, the mini ad from `shop-look.md` (the hero mockup, the product name and 2 or 3 "what's inside" points). Only the banner goes here. **Check that it's made before you send this message.** If it isn't, make it first, then show a preview and save it, like any missing image (Kristin's test, Oct 17: it was never made, and the checkout still showed Stan's sample photo).
   - **Designed checkout page** (if they chose all images, or images and text, in Apply Round 5): the section images go **inside Description Body**, using its image button, never in the banner spot. Give the exact order, piece by piece. For images and text: "Image 1 (hero), then the paragraph that starts 'This weekly dinner planner…', then Image 2 (the problem)…". For all images: the sections in order, then the must-know words as text below.
   - **The copy, as plain text:** Description, Description Body, the Bottom Button Title and the Call-to-Action Button text, each with Round 3's words for that box (`sales-pages.md`, Stan row). Stan's description can't hold clickable buttons or links, so there are no button markers in it. Stan's own button is the only one.
   - **The price.** Discount codes and limited quantity need Creator Pro.
4. **At the bottom of the Checkout Page,** add the product in the way they chose in Round 8 (`delivery.md`):
   - **upload the file** (PDFs and most file types, up to 5 GB, ideally under 500 MB)
   - **or redirect to a URL,** for a Canva view link or template link
5. **Options tab** → **Confirmation Email:** paste the subject and message from `buyer-email.md` (written and approved before this step). Stan adds the product to the email itself. Creator Pro adds Order Bumps, Email Flows and Affiliate Share (`upsells-and-funnels.md`).
6. **Publish**, then open their store link on their phone to check it. Ask them to paste **two links** for their notes: the store link, and the product's own link (tap the product, then copy the address from the browser; it has `/p/` in it). Market shares the product link, so buyers land right on it (Kristin's test, Oct 17: only the store link was saved).
7. **Now connect payments:** **Settings** → **Payments** → the button next to Stripe (it may say **Register**, **Connect** or **Connect Your Bank Account**).
   1. Choose their home country → **Create My Stan Stripe Account**. It's a new Stripe account managed by Stan; an existing one can't be connected.
   2. Enter the phone number and email on their Stan account, then the SMS code. The phone must be from the country they chose.
   3. **Their personal details:** name, email, phone, home address, birthday, and in the US the last 4 digits of their SSN. They type these into Stripe, never into this chat.
   4. Their industry, then their bank account for payouts. Then **Submit**.
   - **Verification:** it can take a few business days. If the Home tab says "Your Stripe account requires an update", they click **Update Now**.
   - **If the button is grayed out,** they publish a product first, refresh, and try again. Pop-ups must be allowed.
   - **PayPal instead:** in the same Payments tab.
8. **Terms & Conditions,** in the same tab: **Settings** → **Payments** → turn on **Enable Terms & Conditions**. Fill in every blank (______) with their details: the terms only save once every blank is filled. They apply to all their Stan products, need a paid plan, and show on the checkout page just above the button. Stan's help: https://help.stan.store/article/87-how-to-enable-terms-and-conditions (checked October 4, 2026).

**The buyer check on Stan:** preview the store and checkout on a phone. Then Stan's own test, on any plan (help article 233): set the price to $0, publish, "buy" it with their own email, and check the thank-you page and the email from orders@stan.store. **Then set the price back and publish again.** Never test by buying at full price and refunding: Stripe keeps its fees. Details: `buyer-email.md`, section 4.

Stan can also add sales tax automatically (Settings). Help center: https://help.stan.store

**Let you build it for them (recommended): the Systeme.io connector.**
Systeme.io has an official connector for Claude. Kristin tested it in her account in October 2026: connecting, the funnel and its steps, the price, the coupon, the product and the offer all worked. **How they connect it.** It works with a personal key, not the usual Connect button (that one fails):
1. In Systeme.io, click their profile picture → **Settings** → **MCP & API keys**.
2. Under **MCP keys** (not "Public API keys"), click **Create**, name it `claude`, and choose an expiry date.
3. **Copy the key right away.** It's only shown once. If they missed it, delete it and create a new one.
4. In Claude: **Customize** → **Connectors** → **Add** → **Add custom connector**.
   - **Name:** `systeme.io`
   - **Remote MCP server URL:** `https://mcp.systeme.io/mcp?mcpKey=` with their key pasted right after the `=`
5. For **Authentication**, choose **No sign-in** (the key does the signing in). Ignore the OAuth section. Click **Add**.
6. For permissions, choose **Needs approval**, so nothing changes in their account without their click.
7. In a chat, turn it on from **+** → **Connectors**.

**The key is like a password.** Tell them never to paste it into the chat or a screenshot, only into the connector form. If they do share it, have them delete that key in Systeme.io and create a new one. Keys expire, so when the connector stops working, they make a new key and update the connector.

**What you build, in this order** (the connector's own rules; call its `describe_mcp_instructions`, `describe_payment` and `describe_funnels` first):
1. Check their payment methods for their currency. **Payments stay with them:** if none are connected, they connect Stripe or PayPal in Systeme.io themselves.
2. **Plan every step first, then create them one at a time, in order:**
   - sales page → order form → (upsell, then downsell, only if they have an add-on for it) → order thank-you page
   - Create the funnel with no funnel type, then add the steps. Steps can't be reordered later, and new ones always land at the end.
3. The price plan (their price, one-time) → the digital product → attach it to the order form's offer, with the test coupon. Never guess a price: use the one approved in Round 6.
   - **Order bump:** the add-on's own price plan and product, added to the same offer (one bump per offer).
   - **Upsell:** its own offer (`upsells-and-funnels.md`).
4. **The pages,** written with `sales-pages.md` and shown to them before saving.
   - Systeme.io's ready-made sales page and order form templates require testimonials (names, photos), instructors and credentials. **Never fill those with invented people.**
   - If they don't have real ones (most beginners don't), explain it in one line and build those pages without templates. The connector allows that once they agree.
   - The thank-you page template has no proof slots.
   - **If the coupon is on:** the order form needs the coupon box.
   - **If there's an order bump:** the order form needs the order bump block, unticked. Its box doesn't show the add-on's price until it's ticked, so start its text with "Add it for ${price}."
   - **Then open every page, on a computer and in phone view, and fix what the builder chose** (Kristin's test, Oct 2026):
     - Its color presets don't come out as named.
     - A "waves" background behind the top section can't be edited later, so ask for a solid one.
     - Card pictures came out as stock photos of strangers. Leave card images empty.
     - Recolor with the editing tools: light backgrounds, dark text, and one bright button color used nowhere else.
   - The checkout asks for a phone number and address by default. Systeme.io uses them for tax, so leave them unless they know they can remove them.
5. **The delivery email:** an automation email, plus a rule: when there's a new sale on the order form → send that email. It goes out under their default sender name (often their business name), so sign it to match.
6. A test coupon (100% off, limited uses) so they can run a real test checkout.
7. **By hand, in the page editor:** **Settings** → **Search Engine Optimization (SEO)** → a title and description with their main keyword. Then check every page in the **mobile** preview.

**The one limitation: files.** The connector can't attach a downloadable file to a product sold in a funnel yet. For a PDF product, deliver it one of these ways (step by step: `delivery.md`, Routes B and D):
- **A download button on the thank-you page, plus the same link in the confirmation email.** The link goes to the product file itself in Google Drive (Anyone with the link: Viewer), or to their thank-you PDF for bundles: Kristin's delivery methods in Round 8.
  - **Use the delivery option they chose in Round 8, and make the pages and email say exactly what arrives.** In Kristin's test the sales page promised a printable PDF, but the button opened a Canva view link: the buyer got a page to scroll instead of the PDF they expected.
  - **For a PDF:** export it for them with the Canva connector. They upload it to Google Drive and paste the share link. Check it opens the PDF, with a download button, while signed out.
  - **For a view link or template link:** use that link directly (`delivery.md`, Route E).
- **Or inside a Systeme.io course** that the purchase unlocks (the PDF as a lesson download). This is more protected, but more setup.

Two things to know:
- The thank-you page's web address isn't secret. Anyone who has it can open it without paying. That's usually fine for a low-price printable. For pricier products, use the course.
- **An add-on's link never goes on the shared thank-you page.** Give the add-on product a tag as its resource. Then create a rule: when that tag is added → send the add-on's email.

**Never touch their existing funnels, products or emails** unless they ask. Name anything you create clearly, and check each result in their Systeme.io dashboard before you call it done.

**Without the connector (the general path):**
1. Sign up for the free plan with Kristin's link.
2. Connect Stripe or PayPal in the payment settings.
3. Create a **sales funnel** whose goal is to sell a product: a sales page, an order form step and a thank-you page.
4. On the order form, create the offer: the price and the digital file (or course) the buyer gets.
5. Write the sales page from the Problem → Solution → Outcome copy (`step-apply.md`).
6. Turn on the purchase confirmation email so the file is delivered automatically.
7. Optional: an **order bump** on the order form, or a **one-click upsell** step after it (`upsells-and-funnels.md`).
8. Publish the funnel and test it.
Systeme.io's screens change often, and there's no Kristin module for it yet. Walk them through one screen at a time, ask what they see, and look up Systeme.io's current help pages if anything doesn't match.

## Gumroad

**Best for:** the fastest no-tech start, and sellers in countries other platforms can't pay. **Tech:** beginner.
**Costs (official, Oct 2026):** no monthly fee. **10% + $0.50 per sale** from their own links; **30%** when the sale comes through Gumroad's own Discover marketplace. Gumroad is the merchant of record: it collects and pays sales tax and VAT worldwide for them. Official: https://gumroad.com/pricing
**Get paid:** to a local bank account in local currency in 100+ countries (including India, Pakistan, the Philippines, Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Egypt), or to PayPal elsewhere (2% fee). Minimum payout $100. New accounts go through a review (often 1 to 3 weeks, usually after a few sales) before the first payout. Official: https://gumroad.com/help/article/13-getting-paid

**Set up (Gumroad's current steps):**

2. Fill in their **Payments** settings (payout country, bank or PayPal, ID details).

6. **Content** tab: add the files (and an instructions page if they want).
