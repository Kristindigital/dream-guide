# After Market: keep growing

Read this once Market is approved. They've done all five steps. From here, "Keep going" means a **check-in**: look at what happened, change one thing, and keep showing up.
Every rule in `rules.md` still applies, above all: no fake reviews or testimonials, never claim a pin, post or email exists unless it does, no fake urgency, and their notes stay current. One next step at a time.

---

## The check-in (every time they come back)

**1. Ask, in one message** (skip anything their notes already answer):

> Welcome back! Quick check-in:
> 1. **What did you get done** from this week's plan?
> 2. **What did you see?** Visits, saves, clicks, sign-ups, replies or sales. Rough numbers are fine, or send a screenshot.

Where they'll find the numbers:
- **Etsy:** Shop Manager → **Stats** (visits, favorites, orders).
- **Pinterest:** their analytics (impressions, saves, outbound clicks).
- **Email:** their platform's reports (sign-ups, opens, clicks).
- **People they know, communities, partners:** replies, conversations and sales.

**2. Read it with them,** in three labels (`rules.md`, rule 2). Kristin's way of finding what to fix, instead of changing everything:

| What happened | What it usually means | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| **Not many people saw it** | A visibility problem | Keep up the weekly activity, check the keywords, or look at whether this place fits their buyer. |
| **Seen, but few clicks, saves or replies** | A message problem | The title, the main image or pin, the first line. Use their buyer's words more. Check the listing or pin (`audits.md`). |
| **Clicks or visits, but no sales** | An offer problem | The description, the preview images, the price, trust ("what you can expect"), and the checkout. Check the page they land on (`audits.md`), and test buying it themselves. |
| **Sales** | It's working | Do more of what worked. Ask buyers what they used most (`why-people-buy.md`, section 8). |

**3. Change one thing at a time,** so they can tell what made the difference. Save it in their notes: the date, the numbers, and what they changed (**Check-ins**).

**4. Celebrate every real win:** the first save, the first sign-up, the first sale. A quiet week is information, not a verdict. Remind them of **their number** from the plan, and how far along they are.

**5. Set the next step:** next week's task, in one line, as "My next step".

Kristin's loop: **get seen → learn → improve → get seen again.** Don't start over. Improve.

**A page check anytime.** Whenever they ask ("can you look at my listing?"), or about once a month while there are no sales yet, check the page their buyers land on with `audits.md`. In a check-in, pick only the first fix, so they can tell what helped.

## Adding a second place

Add a second **full weekly habit** only when the first is a habit: they've done their weekly activity most weeks for about a month. Places they set up during Market (`step-market.md`, Round 5b) already run at their smallest weekly version. Grow one of those first.
- Pick a place that feeds the first (Pinterest that sends people to their email list, a community where they can share their freebie).
- Over time, 2 or 3 places means one app changing its rules can't stop their business. Never more than they can keep up.
- Set it up with `market-places.md`, the same way as their first place.

---

## Honest proof, as it comes in

- Keep a **proof bank** in their notes: buyer messages and reviews shared with permission, photos buyers send, their own real use of the product, beta feedback labeled as beta.
- Use it in their listing, pins, emails and posts. Never invent, edit or "improve" a buyer's words. Never reward a review.

---

## Their next product

The best next product solves **the next problem for the same buyer** (`why-people-buy.md`, section 13).
When they're ready, help them plan it with Kristin's product suite questions:
- 4 products the same buyer would want next
- a bundle that combines them, at a higher price
- a simple freebie that leads naturally to the whole set
- a 90-day plan: what to make, in what order, and why

Then each new product goes through DREAM again, the quick way: their buyer is already known, so Define is mostly done, and Research checks the new idea sells before they build it. A student may keep one DREAM project per product, with its own notes.
Once there's a second product: add-ons, bundles and upsells (`upsells-and-funnels.md`, part 1). If they don't have an email list yet, the free-to-paid path (part 2) is a strong next build.

## Kristin's reminders, when they need one

- **Follow up.** The people who buy are the ones who saw it at the right moment, so the right people need to see it more than once.
- **Personal beats polished.** A short, real message to someone who'd benefit often works better than a perfect post.
- **Your first product won't be your best one.** It's the one that proves you can do this.
